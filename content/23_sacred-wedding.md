My first four visions had profoundly impressed me. Not that I understood their profound (archetypal) meaning at the time; I only became capable of that decades later. But simply because they implied the existence of something that cannot be explained by the usual rules of matter.

I loathed the Hindu speculations that my parents fed on. My father, who suffered from asthma, regularly practiced yoga on his bedroom balcony. Sitting on his heels, unable to assume the lotus position due to his lack of flexibility, he would close his right nostril with one index finger, then his left, and alternate long breaths. It was the only way he had found, after all sorts of medical attempts, to overcome his bronchial spasms. His posture during these moments of so-called meditation seemed contrived to me, and I never stopped mocking him.

My mother, still obsessed with reincarnation, cartomancy, and graphology, accused me of having handwriting bristling with points (no doubt because she couldn't stand my points, sometimes cheeky, I must admit). Her far-fetched predictions never really came true.

Far removed from all that, I tried to rediscover what I called my "channel of visions". For a good year, I drew inspiration from a book on clairvoyance. A certain Cagliari, if my memory serves me right, claimed that simply rubbing a few energy points on the body in a circular motion was enough to acquire these abilities. I rubbed all the indicated spots repeatedly, but without the slightest result. Deprived of François's love, disappointed with my summer exams, and having stumbled upon a dreadful math teacher who seemed to vomit up his lessons, I was beginning to despair. My voice had changed, I could no longer play children's roles, and Mr. Pasquier had become infatuated with a tall student named Marie-Jeanne, a name that I abhorred for years.

From one disappointment to the next, influenced from my time in high school by a philosophy teacher of neo-positivist persuasion, I finally succumbed. Everything had to be explained solely by reason; only matter and space-time existed, obeying cause-and-effect relationships between particles. Everything else was merely illusion. The memory of my visions persisted like an Achilles' heel in my newly acquired armor, but I managed to suppress even the slightest reminiscences until I completely identified with the politically correct persona of the perfect rationalist. Living beings were nothing more than biological mechanisms, and love a mere epiphenomenon. The notion of a soul belonged to pure imagination.

It's true that the relationships my pubescent classmates were having, or attempting to have, with the opposite sex made me feel uneasy. Their courtship, their playing the gallant, their blushing, their sometimes servile eagerness while behind their backs they belittled the girls, the way they recounted their escapades, their supposed deflowerings, the disproportionate importance they attached to them even though their stories rang utterly hollow, devoid of any amorous magic—none of this inspired me to imitate them. On the other hand, over the years I had a series of young friends, with whom I desperately sought that other dimension of love, the one I had experienced with François. This was sometimes the case, particularly with a boy named Jean-Pierre who remained my close friend for several years. It never even occurred to me that our moments of happiness might pose a problem for moralists. I experienced it as if in another sphere, completely independent of the social sphere.

Then came the age of marriage. I married a young woman, Nicole, who had fallen in love with me at the age of sixteen, when I was eighteen. She had remained faithful to me throughout, while I had remained completely unmoved by her most obvious advances for seven years. We both burst into tears during the ceremony, drawing with us the sobs of all the family and guests who filled the chapel. The words of the Lord that the officiating pastor dedicated to us were: "May your joy be complete".

It was complete for three years. I had been engaged as principal cellist in the Zurich Chamber Orchestra, a small ensemble that toured the world, as highly regarded as "I Musici" in Germany and the United States. I saw my wife only rarely. We wrote each other passionate letters. But strangely, as soon as we were reunited, after a few days, or rather a few nights, very repulsive forces would surface. We didn't understand these discordant sounds.

Once I left that orchestra, we were able to experience what we thought should be our happiness as a couple more regularly. Things only got worse. One evening, Nicole said to me, "Actually, there isn't much difference between a married woman and a prostitute: both make love for material reasons, the latter for money, the former to save her marriage". And here I was, imagining I was making her happy...

My first hypothesis: there was something wrong in our relationship. We decided to take a closer look at what was happening with other married couples our age, most of whom were former classmates of Nicole's. The findings were just as disappointing. All of them, except for the brand-new couples, had the same problems: impatience, recriminations, sexual dysfunction, sometimes even broken dishes. So we weren't the only ones facing these difficulties. But what was causing them?

Only one of these duos seemed to thrive: the schoolmate who had replaced François when we parted ways in ninth grade later became a pastor and missionary. Upon returning from French Polynesia, he met a young German woman from a good family, also Protestant and very devout. It was love at first sight. His in-laws balked because he was the son of a glazier and shouldn't have inherited a fortune reserved for the upper classes. Love prevailed: the young lovers waged a fierce battle to convince them, and then, failing to do so, ended up marrying without their consent. They were happy and had many children.

Until the day, about ten years later, the wife told her husband that she hadn't been able to stand him for a long time, that she had done everything she could to play along, but it was too much. That she was going to pack her bags and go back to her parents' house, who were barely still alive. He himself eventually found solace with a very young girl, who seemed to bring him more satisfaction. I don't know what became of her.

In short, the picture of my own marital pandemonium seemed to be repeating itself on a vast scale, Then an incident turned everything upside down. After five or six years, Jean-Pierre, my best and last boyfriend before marriage, unexpectedly set fire to my mother-in-law's cellar. When questioned by the authorities, he explained that it was because he couldn't bear the thought of me leaving him to get married. He, too, had gotten married and was drowning his boredom in alcohol. When he got out of prison, we invited him to our home, the old farmhouse I had just bought in the heart of the Vaud countryside.

It must be said that in the meantime, I had a brush with death from pharyngeal cancer (a lymphoblastic sarcoma, with a 90% chance of dying). As a result, I realized that all the glory and a career as a cellist were nothing compared to health. Faced with the danger, Nicole and I decided, much to the dismay of our family, to abandon everything and return completely to the land. We intended to live off our own garden, to reject all the trappings of civilization and return to primal values. We even planned to return to the raw food diet of the pre-wild age, eliminating all cooking and food preparation, so that what remains of human instinct would determine our choices and rations. The goal was to identify the lifestyle factors that could cause cancer, and I sensed that diet played a major role. In the 1960s, this was considered an act of defiance against the established order...

I needed help to set up the farm, and it was with double joy that I saw Jean-Pierre join me on this path. An invaluable helper because he was so skilled in all kinds of manual labor, and a hope for restoring, even just a little, the magic that had once existed in our relationship. He couldn't stand his wife anymore; this was a rebirth for him too. The unexpected twist was that he fell in love with Nicole, and Nicole fell in love with him. The intensity of our experience intensified. We each had to choose between jealousy and unconditional love.

We were faced with a true "triangular dynamic": when two of us grew closer, the third could, in a way, choose between two positions. Either torment, resignation, and jealousy, as is common. Or, respect for the love he felt for each of the other two partners. The surprising thing was that this second approach allowed the jealousy to be transformed into a feeling of love just as powerful for the love the others were experiencing. I christened this phenomenon "the love of love", or, more mathematically, "love squared".

And that's when a great change occurred. As Jean-Pierre climbed a ladder to adjust a shelf, and I secured the uprights to prevent any accidents, just as his pelvis reached face level, I felt something like an explosion in my heart. A wonderful sensation, with a gentle and irresistible power, that reminded me of what I had felt in the past. I realized that this kind of inner openness, felt in the solar plexus, had been completely blocked for years. The channel had closed as the years of marriage passed, giving way to a purely sentimental and physical love life, devoid of any amorous magic. Suddenly, everything was back; I had rediscovered that extraordinary inner source of transcendent happiness.

Another surprise awaited us: my wife began having visions! Extremely clear and meaningful visions indicated all sorts of things to us, helped us make decisions, pointed out mistakes or dangers. Some even foretold the arrival of a child who would play an important role in our approach to extrasensory abilities. Our lives suddenly seemed guided by another force, ready to intervene at every hesitation to put us back on the path of our destiny... Significantly, the visions also indicated the path to follow to develop them. The phenomenon was coherent...

Jean-Pierre had stopped drinking. He was also making great efforts to clean up his diet. But happiness was too perfect. His wife didn't tolerate his newfound independence for long. She had initially been relieved to see her husband stabilized after prison. She was also very relieved to see him stop drinking. But feeling him so lovingly involved in what she called a ménage à trois quickly became unbearable. She therefore decided to take action and threatened him with divorce. To avoid losing his children (at the time, adultery was no laughing matter), he had to agree to sever all contact with us. He disappeared overnight and never reappeared. All that remained of this exceptional period was a single legacy: my wife's visions… and for me, the conviction that there must be a connection between love and the development of these abilities. But what exactly was this connection… yet another well of mystery to explore.

I will skip over the countless adventures we went through in the interim. I will stick to the essentials, and those who wish to know more details will find them in my autobiography. I will therefore pick up my story a little later, at a new stage in the development of my clairvoyant abilities. This will allow us to better understand the phenomenon and the circumstances conducive to its development (which are described in Bosch's work).

Five years after the Jean-Pierre episode, I met a very young girl named Mirella. From the first glance, she seemed fascinated by my presence. I, too, felt transported back to the magic of my childhood loves. Her mother, whom I met at my first conference on natural nutrition, decided to send her and her little sister, Natacha, to stay with us for the summer holidays. The vibrant sun of the Derborence Valley, one of the wildest corners of Valais, added to the feeling of living in another world. When she approached me, I felt a deep emotion in my chest that reminded me exactly of what I had experienced at the same age with my drama teacher and then with my dear friend, François. My wife also felt very strongly this kind of transformative power of love that filled the atmosphere around us. Her mother, Tunisian and very open-minded, herself fell in love with our love.

I, of course, had a lot of questions about a type of relationship that I had long ago decided to bury among my youthful indiscretions. But rejecting this child would certainly have hurt her, just as the brutal ends of my childhood relationships had traumatized me. Accepting her put me at odds with morality, and even more so with the law, which is extremely strict in this area. Several reasons gradually made me set aside my scruples. First, a deep feeling, shared with my wife, that this was a fundamental phenomenon: if love truly had the function of accessing the extrasensory, then the very raison d'être of materialism was at stake, with immeasurable consequences.

The loss of extrasensory faculties already appeared to us as the root cause of our culture's descent into materialism. The dominance of the intellect and the ego is indeed explained by the loss of the subtle perceptions that open the way to visions and other extrasensory phenomena, the only things capable of putting humankind in direct communication with a higher dimension. This loss, represented by the Fall as described in Genesis—one could say: the loss of natural spirituality—leads to a cascade of frustrations and suffering that can be found behind all the twists and turns of our history.

While the world may cling to its materialistic convictions, the very act of reducing the meaning of existence to biological materiality gives rise to a feeling of emptiness, futility, and profound lack, which paves the way for selfishness, contempt for others, jealousy, hatred, self-interest, mass consumption, ecological disaster, and wars. Religions seemed to have added to this despairing picture only the illusion of a deity reconstructed in the image of humankind, resulting in unspeakable crimes throughout history: crusades, the Inquisition, burnings at the stake, and today, fundamentalism and terrorism. Rediscovering the eternal values that seemed to us inherently linked to love was therefore a matter of paramount importance. Ultimately, it was a powerful sense of responsibility that led us to overcome our fears and choose to follow nature's lead by allowing the child to express themselves freely.

It is crucial not to confuse a love in which the weaker party can express their natural impulses and receive a response from the stronger, with a relationship imposed by the stronger party on the weaker. Morality and legislation do not differentiate, or more precisely, *no longer* differentiate between these two approaches. I had recently observed that this confusion did not exist a few centuries earlier. Until the 18th century, relationships between children and adults were commonplace. This seems unthinkable today, and yet numerous documents attest to it, such as the *Chronicle of Heroard*, a ten-thousand-page work describing, minute by minute, the life of the young King Louis XIII, which I had stumbled upon quite by chance. I later discovered a whole series of historical works confirming the existence of complete freedom in relationships between children and adults, thus justifying what I had experienced in my own childhood. In fact, the very notion of age groups in matters of love did not exist before the modern era.

The sexual prohibitions that seem eternal to us today only took shape after a campaign led by a number of doctors and moralists in the mid-18th century, under the banner of Dr. Tissot's famous book, *De onania* (1750). It should be noted that this pseudo-medical pamphlet did not attack the child-adult relationship, but only the "solitary vice", suddenly presented as the cause of all ills. It was only much later, in 1832, that the first law came into effect in France prohibiting relationships between adults and children under ten years of age. The age of consent was raised to twelve two or three decades later and continued to increase throughout the 20th century.

The only attempt to return to greater freedom, in the spirit of May '68, was a petition addressed in 1972 to the Penal Code Revision Commission by a vast number of signatories. The most prominent were Jean-Paul Sartre, Simone de Beauvoir, Michel Foucault, Michel Althusser, Roland Barthes, Gilles Deleuze, Félix Guattari, Guy Hocquenghem, and Françoise Dolto, leading five hundred other recognized French figures, including many psychiatrists, and supported by another 5,000 people. The aim was to repeal Article 331 and grant children complete freedom to establish sexual relations with the partner of their choice. Unfortunately, this was not enough to sway the justice system. It should be noted that a text has just been presented by the WHO to the UN, proposing legislative reforms that go in exactly the same direction.

First and foremost, we should ask ourselves why the child-adult relationship has been considered the source of serious trauma for the past few decades. My personal experience has convinced me of precisely the opposite. It aligns with the very foundation of classical psychoanalysis: the greatest trauma in life results from the repression of the sexual drives that the child experiences from the phallic stage onward, directed toward their parents or their substitutes. Freud himself questioned the necessity of repressing these drives, known as the Oedipus complex, and the future of a society that would respect them. Denying a priori the existence of extrasensory perception, he obviously could not find the answer...

It remains to be understood why such a reversal has taken place over the past few decades, making pedophilia the focus of all sexual crimes. The explanation could be as follows: as early as 1974, the American Psychiatric Association, under pressure from increasingly powerful gay lobbies in many states, recognized that homosexuality was not a pathology. Less than ten years later, it was even removed from the list of perversions—which amounted to considering it a natural function. This obviously implied a huge loss of revenue. All the care provided to unfortunate homosexuals, the electroshock therapy when they reacted to a homosexual image, and other desperate and hopeless therapies, since one cannot be cured of a natural function, left a large financial void. It absolutely had to be filled.

It was then that Masters and Johnson, a couple of American psychiatrists famous for their laboratory studies of sexual intercourse, had the brilliant idea of reviving the 19th-century credo, championed at the time by Charcot and rightly opposed by Freud: any relationship between a child and an adult inevitably caused irreversible trauma, requiring intensive care and stringent legislation. The psychiatric world, and even the legal system, couldn't help but rush headlong into this new treasure trove. This was despite the fact that psychiatric literature had previously been teeming with positive studies. The most recent one (Rind, Tromovitch, and Bausermann, 1998, APA), reviewing all existing studies, demonstrated that the disorders observed in children involved in non-violent and non-coercive relationships with adults stemmed from the reactions of the family or society, and not from the relationship itself.

However, a new pendulum swing seems to be beginning. A few years ago, the World Health Organization published a rather surprising document. Children are once again considered subjects—not sexual objects—and must be given back the right to discover their sexuality from a very young age, to express their instincts, and to say yes or no in a relationship, without any mention of age limits for the partner. This is, in fact, a recommendation that the WHO is proposing to the UN. This applies to all member states, which are encouraged to amend their legislation accordingly. We will return to this point later.

Let us go back to that summer holiday in 1973, under the radiant sun of the high Valais valleys. Just a year after the letter to the Penal Code Revision Commission, everything led me to believe that the extraordinary feelings of love I had experienced in my childhood, and which I saw manifesting with the same intensity in Mirella, were a perfectly natural and beneficial phenomenon. Indeed, it is generally the deviation from a natural law that can cause trauma. After much discussion with my wife, we therefore took the risk of letting nature take its course, of avoiding, in the name of any principle whatsoever, shattering the expectations and desires that the child might express, and of scrupulously observing the results of this delicate liberation, even if it meant putting an end to it at the first sign of trouble. Everything happened much faster than we had anticipated, confirming that a child of that age can have such powerful yet innocent romantic impulses, and that this type of impulse naturally paves the way for extrasensory perception.

Just after an exchange of extraordinary intensity, Mirella had a particularly long and detailed vision, the first of her life. Her father, estranged from her mother (another model couple!), had vanished six months earlier without leaving an address. Everyone was wondering what had happened. She saw him very clearly, lying on a mat at the foot of a white wall, surrounded by other people lying alongside him, holding a rubber hose that carried the smoke from some kind of brass vessel to his mouth. It was clearly a hookah. Projected onto the wall was an image she described to me in detail, as it transformed: tall palm trees, their fronds swaying in the wind under a dazzling sun, in a pristine blue sky. Then she saw a lagoon in the middle of an island, the transparent water emerald green, people swimming—a peaceful image of perfect joie de vivre. The vision then cut to another sequence: her father had gotten up, his hookah still in hand, and was walking through foggy streets in the rain toward a large gray airplane, whose boarding stairs he used to finally take off and disappear over the horizon.

I had never witnessed such a long vision, more akin to a film than a photograph. Was there a direct link between the wonderful orgasm that had just preceded it and the acquisition of this clairvoyant ability? The crucial question now was whether the perceived elements corresponded to reality. Confirmation came quickly: about ten days later, Mirella's mother received a large postcard from England. Her vagabond husband told her that he had joined a hippie commune in London, but that he didn't smoke. He could no longer stand the fog, the rain, the British cold, and dreamed of leaving for Mauritius to live in the sun under palm trees and under a perpetually blue sky. There was a lagoon there, protected from sharks by a coral reef, where one could swim safely. He had already booked a seat on a charter flight and was due to leave soon; he would give his address once he arrived safely.

I was ecstatic in front of such a series of coincidences. The only contradiction: the hookah. Mirella's father claimed not to smoke (meaning he smoked hashish, as he hated tobacco), while the repeated presence of the hookah suggested, on the contrary, a cannabis addiction, even a desire to seek artificial paradises in the tropics, since he always traveled with a hookah in hand. The future proved this vision correct: a few years later, the poor man returned from Mauritius not only addicted, but also severely psychologically scarred after eating an overdosed hashish cake. He saw himself, in broad daylight, being chased by two demons armed with tridents. Even worse: he was apparently involved in drug trafficking. Perhaps repentant and convicted by a gang, perhaps desperate, almost nothing of him was found after he was struck and torn to pieces in the middle of the night by an express train at the Antibes train station. Mirella, startled awake, heard the sound of the train just before the impact...

During those same years, a whole group of people passionate about our experience had formed around my family. We were now living, fourteen of us, in the old farmhouse, having expanded it by adding many more habitable rooms. Like the triangular relationship that emerged during Jean-Pierre's time there, we had witnessed the spontaneous formation of numerous "polyamorous" relationships, or "triangular constellations", to use a more Freudian term (although Freud reserved this term for the Oedipus complex). We came to believe that love is not meant to be experienced within a closed couple, as Judeo-Christian morality would have it, but rather shared in a much more flexible way, in obedience to the subtle energies expressed by the feeling of amorous magic.

It is perfectly clear, provided one pays a little attention, that love presides over an exchange of energy. Of course, feelings are an important aspect of the process of falling in love, but the main element is not in emotions. Nor is it in the intellect, in what might be contractual, reflective, or objectifiable in a relationship. The main thing, what remains after erotic contact between bodies, provided everything takes place on a sufficiently high plane, is something much more subtle, and also much more profound: the feeling of being filled with a truly nourishing fluid that has been exchanged or drawn from the exchange.

This is not an exchange in the ordinary sense: "what I give I take away and what I receive I gain". The laws of simple arithmetic no longer apply here: what I give, I receive at the same time, and what I receive from the other represents a gain for them as much as for me. Or rather: each receives more than they gave. This kind of exchange is more reminiscent of an exchange of information than an exchange of simple energy.

Indeed, when an ordinary physical system transmits energy to another, the first loses exactly what the other gains. When one battery fills another, for example, the number of joules it loses is gained by the second, with a few minor losses. The sum of the energies contained in the two batteries remains constant (with a few minor losses as heat): this is known as the law of conservation of energy. Energy can never be produced from nowhere; perpetual motion is impossible. Nor can energy disappear without having passed somewhere. Even when there are losses, the initial energy has been transformed into heat, and the total energy remains the same as at the start.

Information is quite different: when two systems, for example, two computers, exchange information, it does not disappear from the first computer. In the second, it can combine with existing information, and from this combination, other, more complex information can be derived. It is therefore no longer a matter of addition or subtraction, but rather of combining information, a kind of multiplication of knowledge, each piece enriching the other.

If we accept that the feeling of fulfillment one experiences after a successful romantic relationship reflects the presence of energy, it is clear that this increased energy manifests in both partners simultaneously. This is therefore a different kind of energy from the material energies described by physics, obeying different laws. However, it shares with the latter the fact that it activates capacities for action. "*En ergon*" means in Greek "that which sets in motion". An electric car with fully charged batteries can travel for miles. Similarly, we feel infinitely more capable, when this subtle energy nourishes us, or when we have accumulated a certain reserve of it, of accomplishing difficult work, reconsidering fixed opinions, grasping new knowledge, discovering new solutions or concepts, and forgiving the mistakes or unkindness of others. Consciousness seems to have been fueled by an impalpable fluid, just as electricity can flow when conductors are in contact.

After a certain period without romantic experiences or sexual contact, the energy reserve seems to dwindle. In prolonged solitude, for example, one quickly feels a kind of inner emptiness, with a gradual decline in the capacity for wonder, patience, tolerance, and creativity. This phenomenon is more characteristic of energy than of information. Indeed, it is the very nature of energy to sometimes accumulate and sometimes dissipate. Ultimately, the most appropriate concept would be that of "energy-information". This information-carrying energy is found in the *Garden of Delights*: the red and blue fruits, linked to extrasensory faculties such as clairvoyance (the owl) or kundalini (the strawberry at the end of the rachis), imply that there is both energy (nourishing capacity) and information (images, truths).

This decoding of the amorous process also reveals another factual observation. Everyone is familiar with the famous Roman saying attributed to Galen: "*Post coitum omne animal triste est praeter gallum mulieremque*" (after coitus, every animal is sad, except the rooster and the woman). Given that the Latin adjective "*tristis*" specifically refers to a human feeling, the word "animal" must be understood as including human beings, as demonstrated by the mention of women. Therefore, what does the rooster signify in this context? It represents a human sexuality limited to that which unites the rooster and the hen, in accordance with the symbolism Bosch attributed to it in his *Garden of Earthly Delights*. "*Tristis*" is also related to a lack of energy. Galen's aphorism would thus mean that after coitus, practiced on an animal level, everyone feels drained of energy, except for the macho man who is content with the pleasure of copulation or the woman who hopes for fulfillment in motherhood.

Observation clearly shows that after sexual intercourse undertaken outside of a feeling of "amorous magic", for example, under the guise of marital duty, a feeling of depression, or even aggression, arises. Spinoza spoke of a profound sadness following sensual arousal. Plato did not wait for scientific advancements to condemn coitus "practiced like four-legged animals", which, according to him, led to the damnation of the soul. It is unnecessary to mention Catholic morality, which considered it a mortal sin, condemning the sinner to hell.

Nowadays, the phenomenon is even medically recognized, to the point that psychiatrists prescribe psychotropic drugs (such as Prozac) to combat "post-coital depression". The question ultimately remains whether it is normal for the union of bodies, and particularly orgasm, to produce a depressive state or a surge of aggression.

Logically, from a biological standpoint, one would expect the opposite: once their duty is fulfilled, the reproductive partners should find peace. The mechanistic explanation that a phase of intense arousal is necessarily followed by a depressive phase also falls flat. Indeed, this sadness is not proportional to the intensity of the orgasm. Rather, it characterizes a failed orgasm, experienced without the magic of love. For this reason, it depends on various factors related to the partners' consciousness and inner attitudes, such as mentalization, desire, dependence, calculation, possessiveness, feelings of guilt, abandonment, or other characteristic manifestations of the ego.

Another questionable mechanistic argument is that, during an intense and lasting romantic relationship, the conditioning through pleasure should produce positive bonds between partners. This is true in the immediate term, particularly in terms of attachment: it is easy to see that orgasm creates a fixation on the situation and the partner. But not when it comes to long-term feelings. If relationships are repeated too often with the same partner, the level of amorous magic inevitably diminishes. We even see the emergence of impulses of rejection, impatience, and aggression. These impulses cannot be explained within the framework of a sexuality devoted to reproduction: on the contrary, partners should remain together for a long time, long enough to raise children, while guaranteeing them a harmonious environment conducive to their development. However, if we believe Reich, the average duration of a relationship is less than four years. This means that love would have given way to boredom and separation before the eldest child in a family is three years old, and even worse for younger children.

Experience clearly shows that it is the state of consciousness of the partners at the moment of romantic fulfillment that is decisive. In a relationship underpinned by calculation, the desire for repetition, regret, comparison, reproach, guilt, or simply desire—in fact, whenever one identifies with the ego—depression automatically follows pleasure, giving rise to indistinct impulses of rejection (turning one's back on the partner), dissatisfaction, and resentment. Conversely, if one finds oneself in a "tantric" state, a state of meditation, letting go, absence of calculation, jealousy, and possessiveness, openness to others, selflessness, and gratitude to the Creator, the sensation of ecstasy extends far beyond orgasm. One experiences a fullness that can last for hours and days, as if one had been replenished with a miraculous energy, a source of harmony, bliss, and creativity.

The most likely hypothesis is that erotic contact presides over an exchange of energy-information when the state of consciousness is adequate, and conversely, that this exchange is blocked when the energy risks fueling egotistical impulses, contrary to the favorable evolution of consciousness. A corollary, then, is that we consider this state of grace as the normal state of the human psyche. It does indeed lead to patience, tolerance, creativity, and harmony. Bonobos are already capable, according to ethologists, of using sexuality to resolve their conflicts. It would be paradoxical if, in humans, it were to naturally lead to aggressive, depressive, or other negative traits.

Let us note in passing that the positive effects of Eros on an individual's dispositions and resources are favorable to the survival of the species: the hypothesis is therefore in accordance with the laws of evolution...

We still need to understandd why coitus should almost automatically lead to a loss of energy, unlike other forms of physical intimacy. If we are to believe his brush, Bosch reserves it strictly for procreation and harshly criticizes the morality that seeks to make it the central pillar of sexual relations. My own observations have largely confirmed this. Sacred unions should therefore be limited to non-penetrative relationships.

Vestal Virgins and other guardians of the sacred were required to be virgins. This was undoubtedly not just an empty tradition…
