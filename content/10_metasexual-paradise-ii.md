A major difference can attract our attention: in the left rock, a horizontal spine evokes penetration, while all the characters are male. In the right rock, penetration is represented only by a curved point without thorns, on which two parakeets kiss.

However, what forms of sexuality do not lead to any reproduction, one of which includes penetrations and the other does not or, at least, penetrations of a different type? Of course, male homosexuality and female homosexuality. We should therefore amend previous interpretations, and accept that the two rocks to the left and all to the right represent the respective provisions of women and men in the heterosexual context, whereas the two rocks in the background portray homosexuality, either male (left) or female (right). We shall ascertain in detail whether this hypothesis, unprecedented in the history of triptych exegesis, really holds true.

![](black-bird-homop.jpg)The woman seen just above the two birds, holds a crescent moon at arms symbolizing the female sex, in light contact with the curved tip. The absence of spine on the curved tip recalls the difference between delicate digital contacts specific to female homosexuality and more brutal stimulations too often associated with heterosexual penetration. Polymorphic contacts appear to be rich in transcendent energy generation, as symbolized by the two trilobed trees emerging from the egg where the woman hides, for both partners (the second is seated at the bottom of the wheel) as the two parrots evoke.

The central panel of the *Garden of Earthly Delights* offers, in the final analysis, a complete overview of the potentialities of human sexuality. The "pleasures" discussed here are understood as going much further than pleasure or tension relief, quite the opposite of lust and attachment. Bosch invites us to a true initiation, scrupulously listing all the conditions that can assure love and sexuality their spiritual function. In our language: an initiation to metasexuality, a path of primary access to extrasensory faculties, they themselves indispensable guide of our spiritual evolution.
