title: "Publisher's note"
weight: 26
---

Born in 1934 in Lausanne to a family of musicians, Guy-Claude Burger pursued brilliant studies in physics and mathematics. Appointed assistant in theoretical physics at the University of Lausanne, he soon turned away from science, hoping to find a deeper meaning of existence in art. Winner of the Geneva International Music Competition in 1960, he toured the world as principal cellist of the Zurich Chamber Orchestra. Suddenly diagnosed with cancer, he decided to start anew and embarked on groundbreaking research into the links between food and illness. He rehabilitated the concept of the food instinct (1964) and discovered previously unknown connections between food, sexuality, and paranormal abilities. Attributing a metapsychic purpose to non-genital sexuality, he founded metapsychoanalysis in 1977, unifying the views of Freud, Jung, Reich, and Plato. Reconsidering mythology, religions, and artistic creativity, he developed an innovative paradigm that challenged the entirety of Western culture. He earned a degree in psychology at the age of 72 and defined "cross-drive" analysis. He introduced the concept of "mythogenesis", attributing the creation of myths and certain works of art, including the triptychs of Hieronymus Bosch, to an original function aimed at denouncing the errors of civilization. His research repeatedly brought him into trouble with the media and the courts, following accusations he always denied. Now in his eighties, he dedicates himself to passing on the fruits of his reflections to future generations.
