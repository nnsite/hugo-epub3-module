I still need to tell you about a more dramatic episode in this unusual adventure, essential for understanding the mysteries of Bosch. After three years of a transformative love, lived in perfect innocence—and perfect recklessness—the unexpected happened. Nicole became angry with Mirella's mother and ordered her to leave. The mother wanted to take her daughter, but Mirella refused to leave what she experienced as a moment of paradise. As a result, her mother sent a letter of denunciation to the judicial authorities. A few days later, child protective services came to get her and placed her in foster care.

I can't find the words to describe the pain that tore at my heart. I couldn't concentrate on any work for more than a few minutes; I had to run out onto the country lanes surrounding our farm, screaming at the top of my lungs to slowly regain my senses. What hurt me most wasn't so much the breakup itself, but the fear of seeing Mirella go down the path so many young people take when they're estranged from their families: smoking, alcohol, drugs, delinquency. Everything I tried only made things worse.

Yet, a completely unexpected event brought me some comfort. Moved by my suffering, a young woman named Diane, who, along with her mother, was also part of the group, wanted to support me. A few months earlier, Mirella had invited her to get closer to us. There had been, between her and us, one of those exchanges characteristic of a triangular relationship, in which each person carries the love of the other two. More precisely: in which the three poles offer their love to the divine. And now, it was as if the energy I'd accumulated back then was going to help me through this ordeal, and might even have surprising consequences for the whole group.

Without quite knowing how, Diane and I found ourselves in bed, as if to commemorate this moment of grace. The next day, an unexpected phenomenon occurred. As she was cycling back from a neighboring farm in the late afternoon, she saw a giant orchid in the fields, a magnificent shade of purple.

We were astonished by this apparition. What could it possibly mean? The answer came quickly. That very evening, Diane began having a surprising series of visions, which showed in detail the situation Mirella was in. All I had to do was ask a question, and a vision would appear and provide the answer. I took out a notebook to jot them down, because the number of images, each more unexpected than the last, far exceeded my capacity to remember them. By the end of the evening, I counted more than four hundred visions.

I felt, however, puzzled by the highly cryptic symbolism of some of these images. Geometric shapes, sumptuous garments, royal crowns, radiant suns, but also skeletal figures, people upside down, others crucified, images of demons, chimeras, zombies, dark abysses... It all seemed terribly heterogeneous to me. Sometimes, the meanings were perfectly clear and were directly verified in reality (as my first four visions had been). But often, we couldn't find a satisfactory interpretation.

I then asked my young oracle—or rather, I asked the visions—to show me how to arrive at the correct interpretation. The next vision was that of a child's toy: A small wooden train, consisting of a locomotive and a series of wagons, passed successively before the observer's eyes. This clearly meant that the visions should not be interpreted in isolation, but rather in their sequence, identifying common denominators capable of providing, through their cross-referencing, the key to the coded message. I also observed that a false interpretation was immediately refuted by subsequent visions: I sometimes appeared in the form of a parrot suspended by its feet, a Punchinello, or some other symbol of error or foolishness. Correct interpretations, on the other hand, resulted in reassuring images: an arrow hitting the exact center of a target, a hand with its thumb extended upward, a king seated on a throne, and so on.

My initial reaction, however, was to think that Diane had some psychological issues that would explain such a heterogeneous pattern. Abandoned by her father, shuttled between her mother and grandparents, she undoubtedly suffered from a certain imbalance that could be expressed by the excessive and often incoherent nature of this incredible symbolism.

Imagine my surprise: a young man, Victor, who was very close to my wife at the time and who, thanks to triangular constellations, had also been close to me in the past, and then for a few days close to Mirella, began to have visions in turn. These visions presented exactly the same types of symbols as Diane's and occurred at the same frequency. The moment I asked a question, the vision would appear. Sometimes, it even anticipated the question.

The similarity of the symbols then led me to believe that this was not an individual peculiarity, or even a disorder, but a genuine, reproducible phenomenon, characterized by universal symbols. This provisional conclusion was reinforced in the following days by the "connection" of another young woman, named Nicole like my wife, the partner of a man who had also been in love with Mirella and had then become close to us, Jean-Luc. The symbols in the visions of this new oracle were exactly the same, only their frequency was slower.

Then it was the turn of a young doctor who had joined our research, Jean. Very close to Victor, he had formed a triangular relationship with him and my wife. The same symbols appeared in his case as well, at a rate of about two hundred visions a day. A very rational person, he was initially uneasy about it, having never imagined such an experience. And that's not all: Jean-Luc, some of my children, Mirella's mother (who had grown closer to me in the meantime), and a total of sixteen people who lived on our farm or who had affinities with some of us, also began to have perfectly meaningful visions, always with the same type of symbols.

The facts unequivocally demonstrated that we were dealing with a perfectly consistent, verifiable, and reproducible phenomenon. It could appear in anyone. As if a particular energy, linked to a certain way of experiencing love, were all it took for this incredible "third eye" to open. The expression doesn't exist by chance. This function was certainly already known to the ancients. It is found, in particular, in Tantrism. Visions also accompanied the lives of the prophets of the Old Testament. The Gospels also speak of them. Our experience shows, however, that it is not necessary to wear a halo to develop this faculty. It should rather be seen as a perfectly natural function, like a kind of meta-visual organ, a transcendent eye capable of seeing into another dimension, which opens in everyone when love is experienced in accordance with certain natural laws and allows one to find the necessary subtle energies.

The primary condition seemed to be participation, direct or indirect, in loving relationships such as I have attempted to describe, and as the *Garden of Delights* presents them in the Pedagogical Province. They are characterized by an inner attitude of letting go of everything that might involve the ego. One must be centered in the solar plexus, and not in the head or the heart. I also better understood why my first visions had appeared just after this period of romantic discovery experienced at age thirteen through my relationship with François. There was also a triangular aspect to this (since I was still very close to Mr. Pasquier), therefore a significant convergence of energies, in the great innocence of a first discovery of the magical dimension of love.

It's as if the energy drawn from this type of romantic relationship directly fuels extrasensory perception. First, to structure it in those who lack it, then to strengthen it deeply and in its symbolic manifestations. Diane, moreover, continued to develop her abilities in a truly remarkable way. She discovered the capacity to visualize the affected organs inside a patient's body and ended up working in a hospital ward as an assistant in establishing the most difficult diagnoses. It is still her job today.

When I met Mirella again a few years later, she told me something I hadn't known: she too, just during the period when these visions were appearing in different people in the group, had seen all sorts of images, which had deeply surprised her. It reminded her of the great vision she had had of her father, but unable to communicate it to anyone, neither in her foster family nor to her classmates, seeing herself at odds with the entire prevailing paradigm, she had gradually buried these abilities, simply for the sake of peace.

Further confirmations would corroborate these initial observations. During the 1980s and 90s, I witnessed the emergence of about fifty more cases around a new group experiment, this time in France. Each of these new psychics had particular predispositions, reacting better in specific areas, but the basic pattern was always the same: symbols that seemed borrowed from ancient wisdom, sometimes translated into modern iconography. And the visions were completely available: one only had to ask a question, and the answer was given instantly.

In 1983, a young girl began to sometimes see letters. These written characters were slightly distorted or had a flaw that gave us clues to their meaning. Then she saw words. The words also underwent distortions, for example, in the form of spelling mistakes. These apparent anomalies referred to homonyms or paraphrases whose similarities with the distorted word allowed us to determine quite precise meanings. Then came much more explicit sentences. These, too, were sometimes altered, by a grammatical error, backwards writing, or some other salient detail that put us on the trail of the correct interpretation. I estimated that this young girl had up to eight hundred visions in a single afternoon.

What conclusions can be drawn from all these phenomena? A skeptic would invoke an unbridled, if not dishonest, imagination in the service of a gigantic hoax. Yet the variety of symbols and the speed of their succession defy the inventive capacity of even the most imaginative individual. It is, moreover, amusing to ask someone who has no clairvoyant abilities to pretend to have visions and describe them. The deception is immediately apparent: just as when one tries to invent a melody, one inevitably stumbles upon existing melodies at every moment, and the listener is not fooled.

Cognitive scientists would see these as hallucinations generated by the brain under certain conditions, with sexual stimulation potentially explaining a particular excitation of neurons. First observation: orgasm is not accompanied by visions under normal circumstances. Or, if it does occur, the phenomenon is rare enough that no one has documented it. There must therefore be another factor. Second observation: once the visual faculties are activated, the visions appear in response to a simple question, and preferably in a state of serenity, precisely the opposite of any arousal.

Psychoanalysts would invoke the release of repressed content, this unconscious content being able to more easily bypass the censorship of the superego when the libido is stimulated. This hypothesis, while certainly closer to the truth, remains insufficient. The symbols that manifest in the various visionaries seem perfectly independent of Oedipal content or other internal conflicts. They can sometimes help to uncover old traumas, and always converge towards personal growth, greater self-control, and better direction of action. Furthermore, they provide answers independent of the visionary's past, and perfectly relevant to the questions asked, often revealing elements that no one present could have known. All of this is difficult to reconcile with previously acquired unconscious content.

Statisticians, for their part, will note that it is extremely rare for such a large number of people around a given individual, or in a small group like ours, to simultaneously develop clairvoyant gifts. This phenomenon continues today: Since I moved to the South of France, for example, four people around me have also developed these abilities. We shouldn't be misled by language here: the process doesn't occur around a single person, but rather whenever several individuals share selfless, non-exclusive relationships. The "connection" happens more easily if one of the individuals has previously been in contact with people who are already connected, so the phenomenon takes on the appearance of a pyramidal transmission.

Beyond the simple statistics concerning the occurrence of the phenomenon, it is the content of the visions that demonstrates that these are genuine transcendent messages. By "transcendent", I mean "that which cannot be explained by the known laws of physics or ordinary psychology". And by "genuine", I mean that these messages have a meaning, if not a specific purpose. The images we receive sometimes convey attitudes or events that would remain completely unknowable through normal perception or deduction. Just as with my first four visions, nothing allowed me to foresee the situations that clearly appeared to me. In particular, the stroke suffered by the tenant of our mountain chalet. Even the best doctor couldn't have predicted it down to the day and hour.

Other examples were particularly compelling. During a long trip to Mexico, the four of us traveled along the entire Pacific coast in search of a mosquito-free beach, where we hoped to develop a tourist center for American visitors. We were desperately lacking in protein, as our natural diet didn't allow for canned goods or farmed meat. Ports where we could find fish were also few and far between. While visiting a large beach, we saw, resembling tank tracks, the imprints of a giant turtle that had come to lay its eggs on the dune. The nest was nowhere to be found; the sand had been disturbed so thoroughly that it was virtually impossible to locate where the eggs had been laid.

Today, I wouldn't do it again. Over the years, I've become a committed environmentalist. But back then, Mexicans commonly sold turtle eggs at the Mexico City market. So, in a state of complete naiveté, and with rumbling stomachs, we decided to replenish our supply. The four of us began vigorously digging in the ground with our hands, lacking any tools, hoping to find a spot where the sand was less compacted. Had the turtle packed the sand so tightly by instinct, or was it simply the weight of its enormous shell? We then took sticks to dig deeper, but they broke before we could reach a sufficient depth.

Having no excavator at our disposal, we had only one solution left: to consult the visions, so we wouldn't have to dig everywhere... I asked Jean to close his eyes while I indicated the different possible locations with a reed. For a good while, he saw nothing, but suddenly, he had a vision of an equilateral triangle with eggs clustered in the left part of its horizontal base. Then, as I moved further, he saw the eggs on the right. I was thus guided very precisely to a specific spot. The last vision had shown a large quantity of eggs deposited right in the center of the triangle. We immediately began to dig.

Alas, nothing but damp and surprisingly compact sand. It was unthinkable that the eggs were any deeper than the fifty centimeters we had already excavated over a good meter in width. Under the scorching heat of the Mexican sun, our courage failed us, and we turned back. As I got back behind the wheel, however, I felt a pang in my stomach. Something told me I was wrong to give up.

So we walked another kilometer along the beach, under the blazing sun, to make another attempt. I ran the tip of my stick over the entire site again. Jean had his eyes closed and couldn't see anything conclusive, only visions of desert, empty containers, and so on. Just as I reached the spot we had already searched, he saw another triangle, this time with the point at the bottom, and the eggs below it. It seemed, therefore, that the eggs were indeed in the spot we had already excavated, but at a greater depth than we had imagined.

I began to dig relentlessly, despite the sun. It wasn't long before I felt the sand give way. I continued carefully and was able to count 83 eggs piled tightly together. Enough to meet our protein needs for the rest of the trip... It was hard to attribute this to chance. All the visions had perfectly converged, and the success confirmed their authenticity.

Another example: Victor had bought an old farmhouse with a blocked sewer. The ground was very hard, compacted for decades. It would have been tedious to excavate the entire basement to find the blockage. He simply asked a vision to show him where he should dig. The answer was immediate. With a few blows of his pickaxe, the old, completely collapsed pipe was uncovered.

Another case, which still amazes me today: An electronic scale we used for selling organic products had broken down. The display board was empty. This severely hampered our business, as regulations required calibrated scales. Knowing a little about electronics, I toiled for several hours to find the source of the problem. The circuit boards were incredibly complex, packed with transistors, capacitors, and resistors. I had no diagram. I finally gave up hope and asked a vision to show me where the fault lay. The vision immediately showed my hand holding a screwdriver and short-circuiting two contacts out of hundreds of possible ones. Without much hope, I took a chance (it could have caused a fatal short circuit to the scale) and placed the screwdriver between the two indicated terminals. To my surprise, the display lit up instantly and showed the exact weights.

I still don't know how this extrasensory dimension can work to give such precise answers in such an unnatural domain as an electronic circuit. But the facts are there.

This reminds me of another example, involving the same visionary (his name was Fabian). We had decided to offer for sale, in our organic fruit mail-order company, banana-figs, very small in size and with a very particular taste. We were planning a first trial run with a maximum of two or three hundred kilos. A new product isn't always a hit with customers, and given the purchase price of these exotic fruits, we risked a substantial loss. So I asked Fabian. He saw the number 997 displayed.

Was that kilos? It seemed absurd. We risked losing two-thirds of the merchandise. Other insights, however, encouraged us to take the plunge, and we had a ton of this rare fruit shipped from Thailand... We had sales forecasting software that indicated the total projected sales for the week after three days. On Sunday evening, the computer showed 997. In the following days, the stock sold out completely, so completely that we barely had enough bananas left to test them ourselves.

Another example, this one very recent: a friend of mine lost a USB drive containing particularly valuable information. We searched everywhere for it for a year. In desperation, I asked a friend, who had also recently developed psychic abilities during a stay with us, to ask where the drive might be. Her answer: in a jacket pocket, somewhere in the house. We searched every jacket and coat currently in use, but still no drive. Then the idea came to us to look in some old jackets piled up in a bundle of used clothes to be disposed of. The drive was indeed in the pocket of an old jacket, though we had no idea how it had gotten there.

Speaking of keys, here's another example: one of my colleagues lost his set of keys while working in our agricultural sector. All searches were unsuccessful. A vision, given over the phone, showed the set of keys on a pile of wood. My friend, unable to bear the heat, had thrown his work jacket onto the wood. The keys he had left in his pocket, which he usually hangs from a belt loop, fell out without him noticing.

These few examples, in themselves of little consequence, have the advantage of providing us with tangible proof of the relevance of the visions. Their frequency, and the fact that no vision ever gives an incorrect answer, proves their relevance. The images received are in no way manifestations of content already present in the brain. They bring us unpredictable information, inaccessible through ordinary means. The question obviously arises as to where and how they can draw this information. But nature is a mystery, not necessarily accessible to our intellectual analysis.
