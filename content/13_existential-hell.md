Right in front of this group, another demon, his beak like a duck's and his arms vertical, brandished a trictrac game. Fraenger sees it as a condemnation of gambling. The stakes seem far more fundamental: when the natural connection to transcendence fails, each event that punctuates existence loses its meaning. Life revolves around a series of coincidences and coincidences that leave us powerless and distraught. We can therefore only accept the hazards of destiny in ignorance and fear, and try to conjure fate like the character who lifts his arms to the sky and fixes the board ready to knock. The three dice remind us that the triangular constellation, a key to non-possessive love, also falls under the dominion of the laws of chance.

The demon, with its big and wide beak, embodies a speech, anxious or threatening as its look. The angle of opening of the beak is identical to that of the mouth of its victim, suggesting that it is the discourse that man holds when he remains only the illusion of chance, a discourse that is established when the transcendent meaning of existence is inaccessible: a disconnected verbiage that ends up denying any metapsychic reality, foreshadowing the dominant discourse that has settled in recent centuries, that of rationalism and reductionism. Bosch was able to see far away by augurating this evolution of culture as a consequence of the loss of the sacred Eros, and it is wonderful.

The logical link is nevertheless quite immediate: while the sacred Eros ensures the development of extrasensory faculties, as experience has shown, its failure places the individual on a plane where the laws of matter prevail. Each unpredictable event can only derive from chance, the psychism's pitfalls escape a coherent analysis for lack of a relevant intuition, material values take precedence over spiritual values. We compensate the lack of enchantment, of enjoyment, of bliss through an endless quest for knowledge, satisfaction, wealth, power. The evolution towards materialism appears as an unstoppable continuation of the loss of the original function of love, a distant sequela of the Fall.

![](sprectral-creator.png)![](young-creator.jpg)Another remark about the age difference between the Creator of the reverse and the Creator of Paradise. Bosch repeats on the reverse the Bible account of Genesis. It is logical to associate first the classical image of the Creator represented by an old man with a sage beard, in accordance with Catholic iconography. On the other hand, in the sign of Paradise, it would have been sacrilegious to display the same old man raising the anathema he had spoken against Adam and Eve. To start over History from scratch, a rejuvenated God fitted better. Only a figure evocative of renewal could announce to the world how to defeat the curse of original sin in an earthly paradise, itself a lethal blow to the Judeo-Christian paradigm.

The problem also concerns young people more than old ones, because the first love experiences are decisive. At the same time we discover a form of redemption possible *hic and nunc*. Here also, only Creator young and christic in aspect, could announce the revolution. It could not, however, be the conventional image of a Jesus as represented with his disciples, for nothing in the Gospels, at least in the way we use to read them, describes the teaching conveyed by this triptych. Neither is the motif of a Christ on the cross, this message diametrically opposed to a redemption through the sacrifice of the one and only Son of God.

Bosch therefore had little choice but that of a wise but young face, Creator but contemporary, Christic but far from the sufferings of the Passion. Moreover, an unconventional figure could only add favorably to the encryption. To the point that exegetes already can not make head or head of this first riddle. Antisexual morality would do the rest, judging frnom the dearth of commentary regards to even the most prominent motives of the reverse panels.
