Perhaps you have never had the opportunity to experience the reality of extrasensory phenomena firsthand. Or perhaps you haven't had the chance to observe that extrasensory abilities are related to love. More likely, you haven't been able to observe the various possible manifestations of these phenomena. In any case, my own unusual experience will allow you to better appreciate the importance of Bosch's message and to better understand how I came to decipher its meaning.

That is why I dedicate these last chapters to a brief autobiography, limited to the elements concerning the acquisition of extrasensory perception, particularly the faculties of vision. As I noted in the foreword, it is not with a light heart that I offer here a glimpse into an intimacy that is certainly disconcerting—if not shocking—for moralistic or reductionist minds. These few pages of personal experience seem essential to a proper understanding of the phenomena in question. They will, I hope, allow for a deeper understanding of Hieronymus Bosch's message, demonstrating that these are not fantasies or superstitions, but very real events encompassing the mysteries of love, transcendent perception, and spiritual life.

Every phenomenon is based on natural laws; the challenge here is to identify what we might call the "natural laws of Eros". These are rather surprising laws, requiring a profound questioning of prevailing stereotypes. My account will show how I came to formulate them and will bear witness to the uniqueness of this unknown world. But one is never better served than by oneself. It is by drawing on their own memories that each person can rediscover the vestiges of their first erotic stirrings, still imbued with magical freshness and immaculate innocence, which are none other than the crucible from which the extrasensory and the spiritual can spring.

The relevance of the interpretation of this imperishable monument of pictorial art, the *Garden of Earthly Delights*, rests on the flawless coherence of two sets of data: on the one hand, the abundance of symbolic motifs that abound in the triptych, which are inscribed without exception within the same overall meaning; on the other hand, the perfect congruence with the empirical observations I have made during my own experience of group life and selfless love. It also relies on the homogeneity of the thesis of sacred Eros, which is based on a few converging concepts: state of consciousness, natural sexuality, innocence, the magic of love, extrasensory faculties, and access to Essences or Archetypes. It follows in the footsteps of great philosophers such as Socrates and Plato, and aligns with the work of great psychoanalysts like Freud, Jung, Reich, Marcuse, and Lacan. Above all, for those who have experienced or may in turn experience the mysteries of this form of love, it rests on the observation of verifiable and reproducible phenomena, such as clairvoyance in connection with romantic relationships.

![][Statue]Ultimately, it is the experience one can have *hic et nunc* of this original Eros that proves decisive. It is important to distinguish the Greek god from the Roman Cupid, whose arrows are aimed decidedly lower. One could also accuse ancient mythologies and philosophies, along with the theory of metasexuality, of failing to transcend certain archaic fantasies, or even contingent beliefs used to justify perverse practices. However, observing *in vivo* that the emergence of extrasensory faculties is directly linked to what this original Eros represented, the orchestrator of primal chaos, reverses the situation. The Ancients were right, and Bosch's work provides compelling evidence of this.
