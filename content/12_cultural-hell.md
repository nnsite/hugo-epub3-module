Just as we read a book from left to right, let's start with what the exegetes call "The Hell of the Musicians". Music was still regarded in the 15th century as sacrilegious, including instrumental music, whose clergy sought to prohibit entry into churches. It would therefore be tempting to see in these musical instruments, become instruments of torture, a simple exposition of the dominant doctrine: the instrumentalists punished by divine justice for their desecrating art. Fraenger himself falls into this trap.

Let us put ourselves in the place of the author of the triptych or his presumed sponsors of the Free Spirit. The message of sustainability must counterpoint the central panel, which presented us with the rules and benefits of the sacred Eros. We can therefore expect to find here all the vicissitudes of a life under the sign of its failure, both from the point of view of the individual and that of society. A society's own culture is its own. But how do we represent culture? An excellent metonymy, for a painter, is music, easier to represent pictorially than literature, sculpture or any other art. It also had the advantage of bearing in it already the sign of the evil, given the Church's positions against the art deemed perverse, of the saltimbanks.

The most insigithful among all the features Bosch assembled this scene with: pointed by the first singer, the score is written on the buttocks of a wretch, crushed under the weight of the construction. Freud would not have done better to image "sublimation". Beyond what we take for the high deeds of our intellect, for the conquests of our civilization, hides in reality a sexual issue. That of endemic neurosis: the impulses that morals forbid, or that no longer achieve their goal, suffer both a repression and a deviation. They then feed either aggressive, destructive, antisocial motions, or activities recognized as positive by society. In the first case, we speak of neurosis, in the second of sublimation. Sublimation may even be the inalienable source of culture.
{.invert}

Sublimation is certainly better than neurosis. It is better to invest in community-accepted activities than in shocking or destructive activities. Rather in piano art or ball art, than in porn or cannon art. Sublimation, however, remains a form of neurosis, of deviation from the fundamental impulses, which distorts the natural relation of the individual to his own being and to that of others. It breaks the subtle balances that allow creative intuition to express itself. It replaces what art could be as an expression of Essences, with contorted personal productions, full of needs of recognition, glory, social rank, power. The need to be loved, or to find genuine love, is converted into a need to pretend, to shine, to dominate.

In this, neurosis and sublimation come together, the first is found in destructiveness, jealousy (very common between artists or scientists), the spirit of competition, criticism, the second in the quest for glory (the real artist or true scientist would be modest, both with regard to his work and with regard to others), in the adulation of stars, in the careerism that parasites all artistic creation. In terms of religion, neurosis is translated into hatred of unbelievers, killing or stigmatizing heretics, witch hunting, repressive morality. Sublimation underlies blind faith, the resurrection of the bodies, a God made in the image of man, the Devil embodying evil. Today into materialism, wage-earning, trade unionism, money, technology, space travel and other traits of civilization from which we hope to derive a happiness we can no longer find at its original source.

All things considered, the musicians' Hell is a magnificent expression of the cultural and societal hell in which the Western world has committed itself. The loss of the sacred Eros condemns us to seek our fulfillment in ways that are not those of the Lord. Spiritual reality, which has become impenetrable, has given way to the illusion of knowledge and power. The grandiose windows of science, art and religion conceil the obscurantism of prejudices and stereotypes that we take for first truths. Mentalization has dethroned the transcendent perception of extrasensory faculties and kundalini, to the point these essential functions became matter of myth and superstition, if not deception. We are, in spite of all our efforts and sacrifices, prisoners of a fundamentally neurotic culture, or better: a materialistic neurosis whose firstfruits Bosch is shamelessly scoffing at in the form of a hybrid instrument unable to produce harmonious sounds in accordance with the laws of the cosmos and a chimeric score behind which hide our sexual and spiritual frustrations.

How to interpret this strange composition? Fraenger sees in the lute the feminine principle, in the harp the masculine principle, united in an erotic duo of joy. Here we saw a redemptive meeting of the once divided sexes, a way to revisit the myth of the androgyn. The woman confined in the instrument would be a beguine, accomplishing what the men could not have succeeded with the alchemical egg or with the elixir of life overflowing from the cup: her godly soul would resonate, thanks to the symbol of the Holy Trinity suspended from her index, with "the quintessence of the world's music". The ensemble would stage the alternative between the musica sacra and the vicious art of the saltimbanks, which the Church considered to be evil.

This rock composition can only mean one thing: the monstrous nightjar has replaced the owl of paradise. Here, no question of ascent to transcendence. The sinner, in the sense of the one who failed to respect the laws of the heavenly Eros, is hurled in hell without even a semblance of digestion: no metamorphosis of the soul, despite the promises of the false butterflies punctuating the sign.

Why did Bosch choose a nightjar to symbolize the Master of Hell? This caprimulgide always had a bad reputation in many cultures. Nocturnal, slamming wings with a whipping sound, opening and advancing its wide beak in a sudden and frightening movement when caught, endowed with a plumage that makes it invisible when it stands still, emitting a prolonged, loud, fast and hard cry echoing in the dead of night, it was synonymous with fear, sometimes still is. It was accused in the Middle Ages of stealing goat milk[^goatsucker]. It was nailed in front of the doors of houses to ward off evil. In ancient times he led the soul of the dead into the Hereafter. Again, the symbolism proves to be more sensible.
 
[^goatsucker]: Hence the nickname of "goatsucker".

A more elaborate interpretation would see in avarice and greed, featuring below, not as parallels to the sin of flesh, but as two consequences of it. The greedy and avarous do not know the fate of sex: only the ecus and the sweets end up in the dark pit, not their bodies. It would be a way of saying that materialism, of which the avarice and gluttony are characteristic features, stems from the frustration resulting from the failure of the sacred Eros: man seeks in material values what he no longer finds on the spiritual plane, of course in pure loss.

One element appears paradoxical at first: the blue colour of l'engoulevent, or even the use of a bird people of the time would confuse with strigids (chulls and owls). In the first panels, the owl was a symbol of communication with transcendence, how could it represent the devil? On the contrary, we argue this choice denotes a profound knowledge of human reality.

When we commit acts not in accordance with natural laws, those of the body or those of the soul, our Subconscious warns us. Even when we are not mentally aware of our mistakes, we feel guilt, often hidden or burried under all kinds of cultural artifacts. But these forces operate in depth, and ultimately express themselves in the form of anguish, frustration, depression, destructiveness, regardless of our inability to define its true origin. As if we became *possessed* by outside forces alien to our true nature.

Now, what is the psychic instance allows us to realize deep down, notwithstanding prevailing prejudices and conventions, if something is right or wrong despite our ignorance of its virtues or harms? Whether it is useful to spiritual achievement, for us or our loved ones? This is where genuine intuition should come in, a subtle function tasked with bringing us into contact with universal truths. Not an intellectual intuition, based on memories or knowledge more or less buried, but a superior intuition, as immanent manifestation of extrasensory perception. In Bosch's symbolism: what the owl represents, with its wings having fully developed thanks to the energy provided by the sacred Eros.

Yet we must be able to listen to this intuition, too often fugitive, and not only tune in to it but accept its messages even if they go against our immediate desires and beliefs. What happens when we reject them, or simply can not understand them? Impulses that do not reach their goal, repressed for too long or left unheeded, or too opposed to common stereotypes to be acted on, accumulate and become increasingly compulsive. Like the water behind a dam, they exert increasing pressure, until we can no longer control them or compensate. Automatically defenses organize, in the psychoanalytic sense we find ourselves trapped in an imbroglio made of aggression, justification, denial, rigidity, securization, which are all characteristics of the ego. Abortion of deep intuitions feeds the anti-spiritual part of our psyche.

Thus, the loss of access to the extrasensory, itself a direct consequence of the failure of love, is at the origin of a paradox which the demonic goatsucker incarnates with amazing relevance: the forces originally meant to protect us from mistakes, when too long repressed, finally preclude any authentic spiritual evolution. They secure our adhesion to the intellect, to anguish, rigidity, hatred, aggressiveness, gradually invading the spaces of consciousness where love, magic and transcendent faculties should reign. We go from Lucifer to Belzebuth, even Satan...

We therefore understand better why Bosch chose this alter ego of the owl, a natural provider of truth as are the extrasensory faculties, to personify the demon. The owl who knew how to see in the night and directed us to the light turns into a dead predatory bird with dark eyes. This demon comes out of us, it is in a way the avatar of our transcendent faculties when the situation degenerates. To adorn in in blue, color of truth, giant as these compulsions are, and unable to ensure our spiritual maturation (absence of digestion), thus proves to be another trait of genius.

Bosch delivers us in a few strokes and dumbfounding accuracy, which would revolutionize all the eschatology: behind what we call the Devil, might simply lie an allegory of the destructive forces that are generated by the Fall in disenchanted sexuality and the loss of extrasensory faculties. It is not for him a demonic entity external to man, but a stupid metamorphosis of the noble functions of being, which are transcendent faculties. The abortion of these essential faculties causes frustrations, anxieties, motions of reproach against the world, jealousy toward competitors, hatred against the different, and other protests of unconscious origin against an abnormal situation, the intensity of which derives from the resulting spiritual failure, and by the fact that this situation continues to become irreversible at the scale of our lives.

Not long ago, science completely ignored cooking food could have harmful effects. It took a whole twentieth century of progress to demonstrate the harmfulness of food molecules denatured under the influence of high temperatures. The most mediatic discovery was that of acrylamide, in 1994, a molecule known for its toxicity and particularly its neurotoxicity, in spectacular amounts in the most common foods such as grilled foods, bread crust, chips etc. It is also quite recently that the effects of gluten (proteins in wheat flour and some other cereals) have been identified, which have a disruptive effect on the functioning of the nervous system. Gluten is a stimulant, just like caffeine from coffee or tea, and cocoa theobromine. At the end of the 15th century, the first tropical products arrived on European markets.

We have the right to be surprised by such a precognition capacity. Yet the whole triptych shows a great knowledge of the mysteries of the sacred Eros and extrasensory faculties. It is therefore quite possible that the role of food has been indicated through clairvoyance or divination. It makes sense thus, that Bosch himself had these faculties, explaining the inexhaustible and unique richness of his symbolism.

From the hell of musicians to the pierced chair, Bosch paints us in vigorous, even blunt, but dramatically realistic, strokes of brush the future of culture when love does not fulfill its primary mission. According to him, and no doubt for the supporters of the Free Spirit, the cause of the damnation of man is not an original sin immanent to him. It is up to us to distance ourselves from the dominant morality and rediscover the laws of the heavenly Eros to escape hell. Evil is nothing but a bundle of natural forces emerged from our depths to call us to order. They compromise our access to happiness and our spiritual journey when we persist in the wrong ways of mainstream morality.

This third part jeers at the Church twice: once by denouncing her morals as a cause of despiritualization and degradation of society. And a second time by brushing the image of the Devil, showing that the so-called forces of Evil are first and foremost forces *against* the supposed evil, in the way of Lucifer, bringing us back to the light. And that these natural forces grow so much as to become destructive when we continue to trampling on the original laws of love, eventually compromising our spiritual evolution.

And then we come to the end of our life as would emerge the undigested prey of an intestinal prolapsus...
