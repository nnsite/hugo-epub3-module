![Enfer_low_res](enfer_low_res.jpg)A thesis does not go without antithesis. To the ethereal promises of paradise life in the central panel, we had to confront the consequences of ignorance, in this case those of morality imposed by the Holy Catholic Church and the Inquisition. As much as the first two panels breathed candour, bliss, wisdom, so much the third plunge into the darkness and torments of an infernal world, cruel reflection of ours.

The triptychs of the time classically represented in three stages the Fall, the manifestations of sin, and the tortures waiting for the sinner. Here, nothing like this: the first part describes a paradise without temptation and without trace of sin, it announces a teaching on the relationship between man and woman. The central panel depicts the implementation of this initiation in every detail. The right panel therefore had to depict not the Gehenna who await sinners after death, but more logically the terrestrial consequences of disobedience to these initiative rules.


![](hell-low-res-egg.jpg)In this respect, this third component is also an education. Like the other two, the first lesson is discovered in the centre of the panel, just above the intersection of the diagonals (grey lines). A huge, shattered egg, articulated on two hollow trunks of trees, plunging their roots into boats, against which lies a disillusioned face surmounted by a horizontal disc where some disconcerting characters spin around a bagpipe.

Most exegetes see it as a chimera: tree trunks form the legs and egg the body with the head in front of it. The right leg shows the exact shape of a knee, the size of the egg corresponds well to that of a torso and abdomen. Thus, the "devil maker" would have stigmatized what remains of man after the sin described in the central panel. However, this would contradict the obvious message of the triptych. Rather, we should find in this third panel the antithesis of the other two: unlike the bliss of the sacred Eros presented as the Creator's will and virtues of the earthly paradise, it should stage the avatars of a sexuality marked by disobedience to the divine laws.

The central panel answered the question clearly formulated in the Paradise question: how should the relationship between Adam and Eve be settled in obedience to the Creator? On the other hand, hell should expose the future of a sexuality that deviates from these original values, and the consequences of this drift on the destiny of the individual and society, in an equally encrypted language.

A detail can guide us: the broken egg pierced by giant thorns. So it could relate to penetration. So instead of a belly, one should see there nether regions. The method of keywords is particularly effective here: hole, inter-leg, penetration, gigantism, hints positively at an obsession of penetration, and as a bonus, in the sordid hold that this egg houses, the model of life that follows from it. In the centre of the broken egg, we see a jug, highlighted by the tip of a penetrating thorn. This gloomy opening would translate into psychoanalytic language by the famous fantasy of the "vagina dentata".

The nostalgic face would then not be the head of a composite monster, but that of the man victim of his sexual obsessions. Bosch did not wait for the invention of the turntable to give us a perfect materialization: three characters, guided by demons disguised as priests, circle around a bagpipe. The instrument that plays without ever taking its breath, smoking with passion, eloquently symbolizes male sexual obsession. The shape of its mouth is itself very suggestive. At the top of another perforating thorn floats a standard whose effigy is also the bagpipe, still specifying the content of this obsession.

![](lascive_face.jpg)he direction of the eyes guides us without risk of error to the subject of this obsession: the broken egg, which replaces the inner egg-unit, implying that the form of sexuality stigmatized here destroys the primary unity of consciousness. This gaze straight out of Bosch's magic brush expresses a whole mixture of feelings: envy, anguish, lascivity, regret, guilt, nostalgia of the past, forced smile, hypocrisy, one cannot better represent the attitude of the one undergoing temptation and about to yield to it while being already aware of the vicissitudes to his destiny his fall will result in. It is not the punishment professed by a religion, but the whole inner conflict between concupiscence and the diffuse awareness of the failure of the sacred Eros we can read on this blundered face.

All this undeniably converges on the idea that the sexual ideal embedded in the prevailing morality, centered on the enormous stereotype of the the normal intercourse and the faithful couple, comes from stupidity and leads to obsession, rigidity, spiritual failure. This view of things aligns in fact with a possible synthesis of the thesis of Freud and Jung: for the first, it is mainly repressing the libido, psychic energy fuelling sexual impulses, that leads to neurosis. For the second, neurosis originates from the repression of "numinous" energies.

If we know the intrinsic purpose of non-reproductive sexuality is precisely the access to transcendent energies, these two apparently contradictory theories reveal themselves as the two aspects of one and the same process of failure: ignorance of the spiritual function of love leads to the repression of the essential sexual impulses, whose meaning we no longer understand, thus paralyzing transcendent energies, resulting in the general neurosis that characterizes today's form of culture. If this view coincides with Bosch's message, we should find in Hell the different manifestations of endemic neurosis.

Let us remember that it consists in the deviation of natural sexual impulses, whose repressed energy eventually feeds destructive impulses, which can take on a magnitude compromising the psychic balance, in particular by altering the sexual function itself. In the hellish lair Bosch painted in such black and blundered reflections, perforated through and through by giant thorns, we witness a scene revealing his intentions.
