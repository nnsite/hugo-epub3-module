If our interpretation is on the road, we can expect the same keys to provide converging explanations to Bosch's other non-Catholic works. It is in this perspective that we will go through another triptych much discussed for centuries: "*The Haywain Tryptich*".
![](char-de-foin-c10.jpg)
{.center}

This triptych is dated from 1502 to 1516, so certainly after the *Garden of Earthly Delights*. We can assume Bosch designed it in extremis in order to provide a further explanation to the *Garden of Earthly Delights*, whose hermeticism must have proved excessive in the meantime. The overlapping of the two works makes it possible to specify the symbolism of the painter and to confirm the respective interpretations. In short, the *Haywain* perfectly illustrates the consequences of the loss of the sacred Eros on the future of the family and society. Under the cover of well meaning moralism, the loss of natural access to spiritual values paves the way for reductionist materialism, in particular the fascination of material goods: this enormous cargo of hay that is pursued and coveted by a host of predators in battle, transformed into luxurious demons as the procession progresses.

![](left-char-fouin.jpg)The image of Paradise offered by the first panel presents this time unambiguously the theme of the Fall. The fallen angels who fall from heaven, the commandment given by the Creator "You shall not eat of this fruit", the forbidden fruit presented by the serpent, the expulsion from paradise by the archangel Gabriel, until the use of the leaves of vines, all is made perfectly clear. However, we will see that, behind these very conventional appearances, Bosch was been able to slip a subversive message that takes on its full meaning in terms of sacred Eros. In ordinary language: "The myth of the Fall and the morals that follow lead the world to hell".

Blasphemy is so unthinkable that it has not been understood for more than five hundred years. Bosch succeeded in bamboozling dominant stereotypes to the point of communicating the indescribable without anyone noticing, save for a a select few who knew better than exposing it. As our analysis of these different scenes will demonstrate, he knew how to be modest enough to let the spectator attribute to pictorial ineptitude the encryption of his message.

It will determined, whether this panel portrays the Fall as a historical reality related by Genesis, or as a religious teaching whose false character must be denounced. The other panels will also have to be examined in this light: what are Bosch's intentions in this triptych, and how does it fit with the message of the *Garden of Earthly Delights* ?

![](tout-en-haut.jpg)At the top of the clouds, the Creator holds in his left hand the sphere of Creation and blesses it from his right. He wears a moderately long beard, has no particular age and surrounds himself with swooning angels, but a little lower flock a myriad of fallen angels, some of whom overflow poor out of this supreme place to fall, like insects in distress, to the earth and the ocean.

![](terrestrial-background.png)In the terrestrial background stands a monumental rocky building. The rock that stands in its head obviously has a double human profile, barely disguised. One deciphers the left of a woman. Her raised skirt filled with water and the two souls-birds waiting for incarnation suggest that she is pregnant. She turns her back on a man, recognizable by his big busted nose. One of the rocky protuberances evokes a massive erection. Above their interpenetrated heads, as a head covering, we find a typical symbol of penetration. Here we have a clear allegory of the couple united in one flesh by the sacrament of marriage.
{.x-30}

![](generously-bearded-creator.png)In front of the rock, a generously bearded Creator, draped with purple and capped with a papal tiara, teaches the original couple the commandments of Paradise. Adam, lying down and turning his back, seems unattentive, or even exasperated by these cumbersome precepts. Eve also seems bad student, her expression glum rather delighted, her right hand's gesture betraying an intent to disagree. Looking closely, the scene is discreetly but undoubtedly caricatural. We must therefore ponder about the real intention of the painter is.

![](snake-char-foin.png)One floor below, the fatidical snake, with a puzzled, bizarrely humanized (although frequently given a human head at the time) expression, clings to the tree of good and evil to offer an apple to Adam. The latter seems ready to seize it, looking bewildered, while Eve already holds one in the left hand and anticipated the Fall by masking her pubic area with a round leaf. Everyone's expressions appear abnormally forced. Are we dealing with the classical biblical language of paintings of the time conveyed by a clumsy brush, or with a clever art of paraphrasing to get the spectator to question this language ? The whole context will tell.

All in the foreground, the angel Gabriel, meaning to discipline two naughty children, chases the sinful couple out of paradise, holding clumsily a sword behind which stand two great symbols of penetration. The two guilty partners hide their sex with a round leaf, with the same impersonal gesture. Adam appears to pose defensively, arching backward as if to highlight his manhood.
![](gabriel-face.jpg)![](adam-face.jpg)![](eve-face.jpg)
The posture of his right arm, the broken angle of the hand and the position of the fingers evoke more an attitude of of spite and justification than one of authentic contrition. Nor does his expression match the direness of the situation. As for Eve, she holds her cheek and lowers her eyes in a falsely penitent expression, her mouth slightly pinched and her neck loose. You might almost find her laughing, or enjoying a victory.
{.x-33 .no-clear .no-margins}

Bosch's mastery, when it comes to rendering faces, attitudes and gestures, is such that we can not decently blame these apparent defects on a temporary disability. Even his assistants would have done better if he had meant to deliver a conventional depiction of the Fall. Must we conclude that Bosch, close to death, suffered from senility and his workshop was failing him... or *a contrario*, that he very well meant to deride the canonical scenario of Genesis in order to draw attention to an encrypted message impossible to deliver openly?

This latter assumption seems most likely. To the extent that Bosch had been initiated to the ways of the Sacred Eros, as evidenced by his *Garden of Earthly Delights*, he could only regard Judeo-Christian theogonia as a mystification. Or at least the expression of a regrettable ignorance whose dominion he refused to perpetuate.

![](dumb-creator.png)![](dumb-creator2.png)Let us also note the burlesque ensemble with which the pontifying Creator in the background appears decked out: a papal tiara reminding us of a funnel and bushy sideburns shortening his face, the whole having more in common wih a costume of Santa Claus than with a devoted iconography. The comparison with the Creator above heaven clearly highlights a deliberate touch of mockery, for example the sphere of Creation in the left hand exchanged for a section of chasuble, the position of wisdom expressing the Logos replaced by a tense hand expressing grenoulage more than divine inspiration. We could see a parody of popes rather than an image of God. It emerges a form of grandiloquence first inspires firt awe, then an inquisitive unease.
{.no-clear .x-20}

Looking at it closely, all of this left panel is full of simulacra of clumsiness, even the fall of angels, which ressemble more diptera, as we would see them in the naive painting of the last century, than the characteristic Boschian demons. One ought to be blind, or brainwashed by the stereotypes of art history, to see there a simple and candid restitution of the message of Genesis. The semblance of pictorial incompetence makes much more sense given a context of clear intention from Bosch: that of denouncing, as he did in the Hell of the *Garden of Earthly Delights*, a religious and social state of affairs attributable to a lack of knowledge of the divine laws of love.

The central panel will confirm this. An impressive hayload advances on a tank, fuelling the lust of a crowd of prevaricators. Hence the classical interpretation that lends triptych the intention to denounce mere greed. It is actually listed in the list of capital sins. Thus, the work would add without ambiguity to the long list of triptychs making up the Catholic iconography of the time.
![](haywaincentral.jpg)
{.center}

The stereotype of sin here obscures the essential: the family, which sits on the enormous load and dominates the whole panel. This is where the key to the message must logically be.

At first glance, the moment breathes family harmony. A father plays the lute to accompany the song whose score his wife presents to him, score followed with his index their young son in red beret. So this is a canonical image of God's happy and blessed home. However, when we look at it more closely, we find that Bosch also encoded blunder in the characters, too skilfully to be chalked up to bad painting skills.
![](harmonie-familiale-haywain.jpg)
{.center}

![](demonhaywain.jpg)First comment: the faceless demon holding the continuo, blowing in a trumpet that is none other than his nose. Probably the same archetype as in the hell of the *Garden of Earthly Delights*: the long nose, as in the famous tale of Pinocchio, symbolizes the lie. This bluish demon reeks of deceit: it carries large butterfly wings, promise of the metamorphose of the soul, but it is a false butterfly. His tail ends with an ocelle, a call back to the sacred ibis, symbol of eternal life, but is thick as a bovine. She starts directly from a sex obliterated under a medallion used by Bosch to represent the loss of subtle functions. The left foot, leaning on a dry branch that evokes the absence of sap, therefore nourishing energy, just like its deformed and concealed face, tell a lot about its musical accompaniment. In baroque music, the continuo guides the other voices: behind the score inscribed with the melody of family happiness, actually hides a demon.
{.right .x-50}

![](legs-gown.jpg)The dry branch draws a parallel with the one the father sits under, and which enters under his wife's robe. The sacred union of the spouses is based on a lack of transcendent energy, the way leaves of a branch grow when the sap is missing. The father's bare legs and nightgown evoke a boring, discolored intimate life.
{.x-60}

![](small-detail-10.jpg)![](obsessed-pir-10.jpg)The symbolism evolves when we look at the couple who hang behind the family trio. This is a man and a woman in a night hat. A very libidinous man exhorts his wife to the marital duty, that she recuses her eyes lowered, a grimace of disgust and embarrassment on her lips. This while a third (which should share the triangular constellation) is relegated behind the bush to a miserable voyeurism. We have here the sexual situation of the binary couple and its satellites, which Bosch ruthlessly stigmatizes in Hell in the *Garden of Earthly Delights*. The family home definitely does not strike as the place of jubilation par excellence for the Sacred Eros.
{.no-clear .x-10-n}

![](small-detail-2.png)![](praying-angel.jpg)A half kneeling angel on the left offers a prayer, at first sight sincere, to the Lord. Adorned with butterfly wings, this motif specifies that the ultimate goal of prayer is the metamorphosis of the soul. But his face reflects more disarray than faith, because a pitcher hanging on a pole intercepts his gaze: all efforts of communication with the divinity, are consumed by sexual obsession (cruche = penetration).
{.no-clear .x-10-n-invert}

![](drying-soul.jpg)The drying of the soul perfectly associates with the motif of the dry branch emerging on the far right of the bush, on which an evanescent owl perches. Even the branch is already almost transparent. The symbol takes on its full scope when one remembers that an owl stood in the left panel of the *Garden of Earthly Delights*, in the centre of Paradise, symbolizing the extrasensory faculties: as much as the sacred Eros can fuel transcendent perception, marital sexuality traditionally based on normal coitus wastes the subtle energies necessary to sustain this subtle dimension of being.
{.right}

![](christ-open-hands.png)The effigy of Christ comments the situation masterfully: at the top of the clouds, a Jesus presents us the stigmas of his hands, his chest still bearing the wound caused by the spear of the Roman soldier. He looks down by spreading his arms, giving first the illusion of a complacent blessing. In the gesture, however, we can see a certain awkwardness echoing those we noted earlier, particularly the closed eyelids. Bosch skilfully lets us decipher, on second reading, anything but an absolution. The best verbal translation of the image could be: "My poor children, what can I do for you"...
{.right}

![](astuce-simpl.png)Bosch's additional tip: the pitcher we noticed before happens to line up exactly with the angel's gaze in his prayer toward Christ: conventional sexuality acts as a screen, no matter their devotion, between men and the divinity, even if they rely on angels to cadge their Salvation.

![](dignitaires.jpg)![](cheval-hay.jpg)Bosch used the great iconographic constants of religion, and family stereotype, to encrypt a terribly subversive message, in perfect harmony with the message of the *Garden of Earthly Delights*. Behind the facsimile of happiness that is the image of the family, or of of the faithful couple happy with many children, hides a disquiet. This malaise betokens a profound spiritual failure, ultimately an civilizational mistake, born of the ignorance of the divine laws of the sacred Eros, scorned by ecclesiastical morality.
{.no-clear .x-10-n-invert}

This good morality, we find it to the very left of the procession escorting the tank. High-ranking dignitaries, monk, St Nicholas, Sister Superior, even Pope and Emperor who exchange the sword of power, all celebrate here in one movement the march of history. Yet, in the crowd rushing in front of them, we notice a new change. Discussions are heating up. A peasant (under the papal horse's head) wears a tuft of hay, pretending to clog his ears.
![](dignitaire-center.jpg)
{.center}

Right behind him, a woman, maybe his, sets off to attack the wagon. A nun even took his ladder to climb higher. To her left, another nun just gave birth to a mendicant, still lying in front of her. His emotional prayer thanks the Lord for the new soul with candid eyes, ready to join the procession. In front of them, announcing a dark future, a domestic scene unfolds in full swing: two spouses fight with cane and dagger for a handful of hay that they tear off from their left hand.

In front of them, two accomplices tried to steal a big tuft using a long, picky claw. Others, between the two axles, follow their example. In front of the group, two jealous women partake in fist fighting, notwithstanding the efforts of the monk attempting to separate them. Below, a murder scene pushes the drama to its climax. The wheels advance inexorably, adorned with equidistant nails evoking the repeativity of conjugal sexuality, bearing all the social weight of materialism, crushing the harassed crippled and the begging monk alike, fallen from exhaustion on its orgastic scale with a small tuft of dry grass under the right hand for only loot.
![](two-wheels-hay.jpg)
{.center}

It would be misguided to see in this tohu-bohu the manifestations of avarice, as most interpreters do. The heart, well highlighted in an empty area, leaves doubt as to the content of these deadly forces: we must look for them at the very top of the chariot, where marital love reigns.

![](demon-sex-fish.jpg)Where's all this hard work going? We shall decipher the answer at the vanguard of the procession. A sort of disillusioned satyr has its belly embedded in a metal corset dotted with pearls (repetitive orgasms), his right leg trapped in a dried tree trunk (coitus empty of energy), his sex obliterated by an umbilized medallion (loss of subtle eroticism), he clings to a dry branch coming out of the top of his spine as an ultimate guide (as opposed to the strawberry that symbolized energy in the motif of the kundalinî of the *Garden of Earthly Delights*), while he gazes towards the earth (matter).
{.center}

![](fish-demon-turban.png)![](sex-demon-head.png)Even clearer, yet, the cause of the detour: before him, a demon, his tunic also constellated with pearls up to the thighs and train, advances by exhibiting a giant ithyphallic fish with prurient features, embodying its form of sexuality. The character's slimy profile reflects exactly the fish's while its train punctuated with pearls parallels a long lateral fin. Its thigh engages in a slit with vaginal appearance, betraying its involvement in genital sexuality.

No better image than this could illustrate how the morality specific to the nuclear family model leads to both the cult of matter (the hay) and sexual obsession, and the inevitable race towards a demonic world it prompts. For Freud, the refoulement of natural sexual impulses, precisely those belonging to non-reproductive sexual function (so to the sacred Eros), lied at the source of neurosis, and neurosis at the root of the destructive impulses wreaking our society.

![](cerf-tool.png)Panther, deer, demon disguised as gorilla and other monsters express the omnipresence of animality, deceit and all-consuming hatred. The queer presence of a young ram with a deer-like look might suggest this infernal procession could include a certain tenderness. This may be surprising, but true enough, tenderness can indeed serve two purposes. On the sacred level, extreme delicacy is indispensable, and moreover perfectly spontaneous. Nevertheless, behind tender words, such as those the husband seems to speak with, standing behind the family trio to obtain the favors of the wife (at the top of the hay chariot), often camouflages a desire focused on carnal pleasure, inherently destructive of subtler love.
{.right}

The arrows of love can no longer awaken feelings, for lack of a heart, let alone amorous magic, for lack of solar plexus, so they land directly in the buttock (observe the shaded area correctly positioned relative to the long legs). The sword stands for the evils of the mind and its sharp aspects, hence ends up turning against the individual himself.

From a cave erupts an agitated crowd heading towards the hay, some already holding a heavy bale. The reason for this eagerness comes to us following the top of the pattern. Several penetration symbols leave no doubt: the dry branch passing through the head of a phallic fungus, the rectangular board engaged in a rounded cavity, the large dried branch carrying the meaning of both penetration and loss of transcendent energy.

![](mushroom-formation-10.jpg)This central panel spells out the nature of the evil afflicting our society: on the hay tank, the bank image of the family model, on its left the sexual error (the pitcher and the lance), on its right the loss of extrasensory (disappearance of the owl). The intention of the painter shines in broad day light: he meant not to stigmatizing avarice in and of itself, as one might think at first, but to denounce a moral leading to sexual obsession and the loss of transcendent perception, with the cascade of ills they entail at the individual and social level. The overinvestment of material goods being the most salient and universal example, it deserved a triptych by itself.
