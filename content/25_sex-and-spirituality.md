The most valuable function of extrasensory messages is existential and spiritual, if that word still means anything. Whenever a difficult choice arises, a choice related to fate, a hesitation between several possible paths, or uncertainty about the resolution of a conflict, the ability to foresee is paramount.

Due to our upbringing, we are accustomed, in every situation, to relying on our intellect to weigh the pros and cons. In simple problems, where we possess all the necessary information, intelligence allows us to predict the outcome of a given decision. It is part of our nature; we would be wrong not to use it.

But experience shows that its scope is very limited. The problems posed by existence, human relationships, romantic relationships, even business relationships, are most often far more complex and unpredictable than a problem of logic or arithmetic. Certain variables escape our control, and all sorts of unforeseen events can change the situation. We can never know the true intentions of our partners, and we often delude ourselves about our own capacity to act. In these cases, by far the most numerous, our "intelligent" decisions most often lead to glaring errors, the consequences of which we pay dearly.

Faced with the difficulty of making the appropriate decision, we also seek guidance in our emotional sphere: "I have a feeling that…", and we rush into action. We have the impression that our emotions will provide an answer where our intellect is stumped. When we realize we've gone astray, we prefer to blame an external event, chance, an accident, or someone else, in order to preserve at all costs the confidence we place in our judgments and feelings.

The overvaluation of the mental and emotional realms is easily explained: most of us have lost touch with the higher dimension of being, the very one that produces true visions and intuitions. And the messages that emanate from this dimension take shape through two channels: the intellect and emotions. The first allows us to formulate them, the second gives them a certain flavor and spurs us to action. Lacking extrasensory perception, we fall back on our channels and obscure the source. We simply find security in what little remains.

This explains the birth of rationalism and its apogee in neo-positivism, and, following the inner void engendered by hyper-intellectualization, the sentimental overinvestment of the Romantic period. These historical tribulations appear as so many avatars of neurosis, whose definition is precisely the diversion of energy linked to natural drives toward secondary drives, which then take up far too much space.

The symbols delivered by visions are always imbued with a certain uncertainty. They draw their richness and usefulness from this instability. An element of enigma prompts reflection more than a simple assertion. The messages are always structured in such a way that understanding their meaning requires a certain "letting go" of our ego-driven impulses. We must relinquish convictions, beliefs, intentions, judgments, desires, and anxieties. Thus, visions and other extrasensory perceptions lead us to progress, to explore our existential space, to measure our inner freedom, and to rediscover our true spiritual dimension, made of innocence and bliss.

When we manage to do this inner work, the true meaning of the extrasensory messages can finally emerge. We are then consistently captivated by the precision, relevance, and depth of the symbolism through which they are expressed. We realize that these messages are like a constant guide, helping us, so to speak, to decipher our destiny, to understand the transcendent meaning of earthly situations, to find the path that should be ours, and to let go of what is holding us back. To draw our existence into the infinitely complex machinery of individual destinies and their countless interactions.

In the absence of extrasensory guidance, all the constraints of the system—obligations, prohibitions, temptations, sanctions, stereotypes, prejudices, desires, false satisfactions, threats, and fears that punctuate our lives—make us victims of what Bosch masterfully depicts in his Inferno. We remain prisoners of all sorts of vicious circles, unknowingly making grave errors in our judgments, decisions, and alliances, embarking on paths that are not truly our own. We no longer truly know where to turn. We confuse our mental speculations and emotional inclinations with what genuine intuition should be.

On a spiritual level, the situation is even worse. We no longer know what true spirituality is. We no longer perceive, or only very incompletely perceive, our own spiritual sphere. We confuse it with religion, morality, faith, the decor of churches, the light from stained-glass windows, the flames of candles, the echoes of Romanesque or Gothic vaults, the glitter on a Christmas tree. Sometimes with yoga techniques, the grandeur of a pyramid, the harmony of a musical work, the benefits of meditation. Of course, all of this revolves around what spirituality should be. The danger is that we get stuck there, going around in circles, imagining we've arrived.

The danger also lies in the fact that, with each act of inner work undertaken with the intention of progressing, we surreptitiously feed our own ego. Telling ourselves, "I have overcome my bad habits", "I have reached a milestone in my life", "I am more spiritually advanced", can be a path the ego takes to seize the progress achieved. Thus, in esotericism and religions, a kind of shift in values occurs very easily, replacing letting go: certain things are renounced in the name of morality, virtue, or belief, but at the same time, we tell ourselves that we have progressed, and the energy devoted to this renunciation insidiously feeds the ego, further enlarging the greatest obstacle to our spiritual evolution. We ultimately find ourselves trapped in a facade of hollow beliefs, Christian morality, esoteric doctrines, or even superstitions, even more mired in the labyrinth of the ego than if we had done nothing at all.

Extrasensory messages, however, intervene at just the right moment to prevent this kind of drift. Provided we are willing to sincerely question the validity of each option or motivation, the symbols we receive are always arranged to help us see clearly within ourselves. Someone who prides themselves on having evolved will receive, like a reflection in a mirror, the image of a puppet on a cardboard throne, or that of a rooster standing on its spurs, or that of a proud rider on a donkey with a devil's head, and so on. The iconography from which these visions draw is infinitely varied and often very direct. I am thinking of a man who prided himself on practicing Tantra, to whom a vision of Diana presented the image of a defecating penis.

Bosch's symbolism provides a masterful example of the diversity and unexpected, even incongruous, yet always meaningful nature of extrasensory messages. For those familiar with these phenomena, it is clear that his genius drew directly from this transcendent source, infinitely richer and more fertile than ordinary imagination. This explains the astonishing diversity of motifs that populate his large triptychs, their striking originality, and the profound resonance felt even by the uninitiated, though without fully grasping their mystery. One reason to believe that this was the great painter's inspiration can be found in some of his works that directly allude to clairvoyance, such as *Saint John the Evangelist on Patmos* or *Visions of the Afterlife*. It is worth noting the absence, in this Afterlife, of any image of God or Christ, which suggests that Bosch could experience clairvoyance independently of the Christian religion.

Carl Gustav Jung, a disciple and critic of Freud, devoted a significant portion of his life to the study of these symbols. He was made aware, through a Chinese work, *The Mystery of the Golden Flower*, of the immemorial existence of invariant symbols, which he named "*Archetypes*". He specifies that Archetypes are similar to instincts, that they manifest themselves through thoughts and emotions, and have a driving and transformative function on the individual. It is crucial not to confuse this with the trivial notion of archetype used, for example, by Voltaire to designate commonplaces, points of reference specific to a culture, shared by all members of a society or resulting from a shared history.

The characteristics of the Archetypes described by the Swiss psychoanalyst are precisely those of the symbols revealed by visions. They are never neutral, but always laden with meaning, relating to the individuals and themes involved. Their meanings are not discovered with a simple click. They are more like Russian nesting dolls, one inside the other. There are always several levels of interpretation, and integrating them in successive stages, recognizing within oneself at increasingly deeper levels the conditioning, tendencies, and stigmatized or accepted stances, allows the individual to progress more effectively than any esoteric method could.

Jung speaks of this inner work as a "process of individuation", in the sense that Archetypes, eternal and universal values, induce profound and beneficial transformations in the individual's life, linked to their own unique destiny. Archetypes prove to be the driving force behind all authentic spiritual evolution.

Jung locates them in what he calls the "*Collective Unconscious*". This term, too, is prone to confusion: the layperson might initially think of it as a set of contents present in the ordinary unconscious—beliefs, aspirations, stereotypes—common to all individuals forming a community. This collective unconscious would thus be the simple product of a shared social life, in which individuals share and internalize the same values. According to Jung, however, the Collective Unconscious pre-exists society, culture, and perhaps even Life itself. It has a cosmic dimension, like a transcendent space populated by eternal values accessible through dreams and visions, guiding our destinies as they may have guided Evolution.

We find a perfectly equivalent notion in the "*Essences*" as described by Plato. They too are located "*above the vault of heaven*". They originally served as nourishment for souls and gods, but souls fell into the miasma of matter and lost access to these primal values. Only love—not just any love: only *Uranian Eros*, or *celestial Eros*—allows the "*wings of the soul*" to grow, that is, to develop the faculties that enable it, as is the case with seers, priests, and poets, to access the lost Essences. These are the source of all that embodies "*Truth, Goodness, and Beauty*".

Far from a Manichean dichotomy, Archetypes or Essences allow us to distinguish, with all the desirable nuances, Good from Evil—that is, what is favorable or unfavorable in relation to our destiny and that of others—in a way that transcends mental considerations, prejudices, or sophisms, which are always arbitrary and monolithic.

We realize the extent of the misunderstanding when interpreters of the great philosopher translate "*Essences*" as "*ideas*": they substitute perfectly banal mental processes, such as concepts or dogmas, for these values of a transcendent, spiritual, or divine nature. From a Platonic or Jungian perspective, our thoughts and feelings should be the expression of Essences or Archetypess, manifesting higher wills or truths, always shifting according to the vicissitudes of earthly life. The very fact that we come to formulate errors or harbor ill intentions, to desire to do evil, to rejoice in destroying lives, for example, during a war, demonstrates that the mind and emotions can stray from this primary function to the point of serving its exact opposite.

Jung specifies that these notions of Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious have been misunderstood by his detractors, and even by his own disciples. The main difficulty in understanding lies in the fact that Archetypes are expressed through symbols, perceived extrasensorially. The layperson then tends to attribute primacy to the perceived symbol, as when analyzing an ordinary image, whereas the essential meaning is more subtly revealed. The symbol is, in itself, merely the vehicle of a transcendent content, which by its very nature surpasses the intellectual analysis we tend to make of it. It is even counterproductive to dwell on the symbol itself. A purely intellectual approach is a source of confusion, of fixation on hasty interpretations. Jung seems to have internalized these values but failed to communicate them, hence his profound disappointment at the end of his life.

Plato fared no better. A chronological reading of his Dialogues, assuming their dating is accurate, reveals a clear disillusionment. "*Symposium*" and "*Phaedrus*" demonstrate a complete faith in the mission of sacred Eros, while his last dialogue, "*Laws*", appears as a scathing critique of what becomes of society when love no longer fulfills its spiritual function. Lacking guidance from the Essences, only opinion and sophistry remain to establish laws and make decisions. To this, he adds last-minute sexual prohibitions.

Already in "*Republic*", Plato denounces the dilemma to which politics leads in the absence of a connection to the Essences: either tyranny or democracy. Politics still presents us with the spectacle of this double impasse today. Neither absolute power nor the right to vote makes people truly happy. The new epidemic of "*fomo*" on the internet directly testifies to the feeling of existential failure that prevails in our Western world.

Plato proposed entrusting the governance of states to "*philosophers*" as a solution, but by *philo-sopher* he meant more someone who finds wisdom through celestial Eros than someone who simply loves wisdom, as is generally taught. Our distinguished Hellenists would benefit from rereading these texts with the understanding that Essences and extrasensory faculties ("*wings of the soul*") are indeed realities, which should be accessible to everyone.

The rediscovery of a link between visions or other transcendent perceptions and the process of love experienced in accordance with natural laws overturns many preconceived notions. It restores the fundamental unity between physical experience and the spiritual potential of human beings. It reinstates a primary common denominator among individuals, allowing us to define, through its internal and shared action, a spontaneous and deeply felt harmony capable of uniting the community.

It implies a new cultural paradigm, fundamentally challenging rationalist and religious options, with all that stems from them: creeds of every kind, materialism, the love of objects and money, capitalism, politics, the very notion of power, education, sexual morality, etc. This is not to deny the usefulness of rationalism, nor of a certain religiosity, nor the usefulness of politics, democracy, or the education system in its current state. These societal elements, which we believe derive from human nature, nevertheless appear as the all-too-often deleterious results of a situation distorted from the outset by the loss of natural Eros. Like so many attempts at correction or compensation, they are quick to turn into traps.

To put it more academically, let us posit that physical love comprises two independent functions: on the one hand, a series of biological, mental, and emotional drives, inherited from the animal world and aimed at procreation; on the other hand, a set of more subtle drives, organized in a more complex way, endowed with a specifically spiritual function through the development of extrasensory faculties and access to the Archetypes.

It is nevertheless necessary to distinguish between two modes of procreation: a purely biological conception, as can be the case in rape, artificial insemination, contraceptive failure, or, more commonly, in a sexual relationship aimed solely at pleasure, devoid of transcendental dimension or the magic of love. This type of conception, which can be described as genital, is not driven by a higher energy, but rather generates unconscious oppositions, such as feelings of guilt, fear, and rejection, which will not help the unity of the couple or the healthy development of the child.

Conversely, a conception governed by subtle energies presides over the union of bodies and imbues it with a magical dimension, then supports the entire gestation and welcoming of the baby, right up to the child's upbringing. Such a conception—which is none other than natural conception—is generally heralded by visions that simultaneously reveal the destiny of the new being, its integration into the lives of the parents or the group, sometimes even indicating its name.

It is understandable that genital conception is frequently experienced as an accident and that it leads to a blockage of maternal and paternal instincts. While the "connected" conception wins the parents' full support, thanks to the transcendent experience of love and the meaning given to the arrival of the new being, this is a form of procreation that can be considered sacred since it is entrusted to us by a higher power. Christians can draw a connection with the dogma of the "*Immaculate Conception*", a birth willed by God and devoid of all sin, the announcement of which was made to Mary through a vision (an "apparition" of the Archangel Gabriel revealing to her that she would be the mother of Jesus).

One might also ask the following question: is using genital sexuality to find pleasure while rejecting its natural function of procreation—a practice more common than ever thanks to modern means of contraception—not in itself a transgression of natural laws? Ultimately, it is a way of manipulating biological instincts by diverting them from their natural purpose. Gurdjieff saw this as a major cause of the calamities that befall humanity. It's worth noting that the Hebrew word translated as "sin" etymologically means "*deviation*" or "*misfire*": love, which should lead either to procreation or to the spiritual evolution of the individual, is instead squandered on pleasure.

This helps us better understand why morality has declared sex anathema. The Catholic notion of "*mortal sin*" likely developed, consciously or unconsciously, to denounce a violation of natural laws, the deleterious spiritual consequences of which were observed by certain particularly sensitive dignitaries. Aiming for pleasure and fixing this attitude through orgasm allows the ego to flourish with all its negative aspects: greed, avarice, a thirst for power, loss of natural empathy, and so on. This anathema, lacking a clearer categorization, simultaneously vilified the transcendent function of sexuality by generalizing to all sexual activity. The Christianized world thus fell from Charybdis to Scylla, with the new pitfall being the impossibility of accessing transcendent values, the overinvestment in objects, money, intellect, and dogma—in other words, materialistic neurosis. With its corollaries: reductionist science, conflicts of opinion, religious wars, the struggle for power, mass consumption, and the destruction of the planet.

As a secondary point, most religions teach us that God is Love. However, observation of nature shows us that Love is largely expressed through Sex. The logical conclusion is therefore that Divinity can be expressed through Sex. One might think this is a tasteless joke. Yet this is the very essence of the teaching in Bosch's great triptychs. If we are to believe Fraenger, he was directly inspired by the teachings of the Free Spirit, for whom Adamic love allowed one to attain divinity. This is therefore an option to be taken very seriously...

A word also on the notion of "divinity". Even in Latin, the proximity between "divinitas" (divinity) and "divinatio" (divination) is striking. The ancients saw a direct link between divination, an art that calls upon extrasensory faculties, and divinity, that is, the very essence of God. In modern parlance, extrasensory faculties are called "paranormal", a term that evokes a violation of the laws of physics, or even a reflexive distrust of anything out of the ordinary. The spiritual function of the extrasensory is excommunicated from the outset. Even though the Prophets and Christ manifested these faculties, they were condemned from the Middle Ages onward under the pretext of witchcraft, perhaps because the Catholic Church saw them as unfair competition to the miracles of its saints, or simply because their mysterious nature frightened popular common sense.

Another symptomatic shift: people used to speak of the "*sacred phallus*". The expression was still found in the writings of many exegetes half a century ago. It seems that in just a few generations, morality has become so sanitized that today this concept is completely erased. It was, however, very present in Antiquity, and this deserves reflection.

Today, caught between the burden of guilt imposed by morality and all the degrading imagery of pornography, we can scarcely consider things objectively. Restoring this primal innocence is nevertheless essential if we wish to rediscover lost values.

The god Hermes was the herald, the messenger between gods and men, the intermediary between the world of the dead and the world of the living. Capable of divination, he intervened in dreams, distributed luck and riches, and was endowed with wings on his feet or shoulders. He, too, was represented in numerous ithyphallic forms, in Athens and in many other Greek cities. His main attribute, the caduceus, was originally a magic wand: a staff bearing a revealing hump in its upper part, capable of turning everything it touched into gold.

But what faculty ensures communication between humans and the realm of the gods, where the Essences reside, capable, through divination, of transforming our destinies? Hermes undoubtedly appears as the personification of extrasensory faculties, the link between our world and that of the Essences. The great sexual freedom of the ancient Greeks may explain their awareness of the transcendence of Eros and its inscription in theogony. Even among the Romans, one could find a whole series of ithyphallic deities or semi-deities.

In Japan, two temples dedicated to this unthinkable deity still exist. One, named Tagata Jinja, was built at the beginning of the 16th century in the city of Inuyama, north of Nagoya. The unassuming temple houses the object of its fame: a two-meter-long wooden phallus, the star of seasonal phallic festivals well-known in the Land of the Rising Sun. It is said that American tourists watching the procession blush and avert their eyes, while Japanese men and women innocently enjoy the spectacle, all the while sucking on phallic-shaped ice cream cones.

In India, too, one can still visit, provided one is not a woman, temples crowned with clusters of one hundred and eight stylized phalluses. A source of incomprehension for Westerners, they seem to be part of the landscape for Hindus and inspire in them a respect for the sacred. Customs and monuments of this nature certainly did not arise in honor of lust. It is clear that they were linked to a sacred dimension that eludes us Westerners.

This shows us how crucial it is to avoid considering the criteria of our morality as immutable and universal values. If we wish to possess the necessary intellectual freedom to grasp Bosch's message, we must take a step back. Without necessarily embarking on a cruise to Japan or India, it's worthwhile to explore, at least on paper or online, what history and geography can reveal to us.

In any case, we will benefit from shedding the prejudices and stereotypes of a culture incapable of understanding the spiritual function of love, a culture that has turned sex into an object of shame and guilt. Innocence is a necessary prerequisite for reconstructing our understanding of the erotic function, exhuming it from the phenomenal mass of disgust, opprobrium, and demonization of all kinds under which our history has buried it. Fortunately, we can do this based on real facts, verifiable by everyone, such as the exchange of metasexual energy and extrasensory messages. In the *Garden of Earthly Delights*, we find the rules we simply need to follow.

It's worth noting that attachment to morality is one of the tricks the ego plays on us. Regaining the freedom to think and act despite prevailing pressures and beliefs represents a particularly difficult, all-encompassing letting go, involving self-image, attachment to parental figures, social dependence, the fear of going it alone, and many other intrinsic components of our Western identity.
It is at this price that we will give ourselves a chance to deeply understand the teachings of the *Garden of Earthly Delights* and the adjacent triptychs, to rediscover the forgotten keys to a universe that eludes us, beginning with the original function of Love, an irreplaceable source of creative inspiration, peace, and bliss.

New Paradigm

Many other thorny questions come to mind: if extrasensory abilities are a natural outcome of love and sexuality, why are they so rare? Everyone experiences love, to varying degrees, from puberty onward. This should guarantee the "extrasensory connection" of a large portion of the population. However, clairvoyance and other forms of mediumship are far more the exception than the rule.

This paradox relates to the state of sexuality in our culture. Freud, despite the pansexualism for which some have criticized him, was able to denounce what, according to him, is the primary source of psychosexual disorders: the Oedipus complex, a period (between two and five years old) during which the child experiences the greatest trauma of their entire life. They fall in love with one parent and feel rejected by the other. The frustration and suffering, comparable to those that result in adulthood from the loss of a love object, are then inscribed in their young unconscious and determine their future psychosexual development. Their sexuality is permanently altered; this is the origin of endemic neurosis.

While this model can be accused of oversimplifying reality, Even today, leading psychoanalysts consider the Oedipus complex to be a universal one. Infantile drives clash head-on with morality, particularly the taboo of incest, which is considered sacrosanct. Moreover, any transgression is punishable by particularly severe penalties in most countries of the world. Thus, the Oedipal phase appears necessarily destined for repression, and no alternative can be considered.

Whatever one might say on this subject, we are faced with a fundamental contradiction. We call normal a situation in which a part of the natural sexuality inherent in human beings cannot be freely expressed. The drives associated with it are doomed to failure from the earliest childhood and can only be reactivated with difficulty later in life. Yet, everything suggests that these are precisely the most precious and powerful drives, those that belong not to reproduction, but to sacred Eros.

The drives that arise in adolescence are of a different nature. They are associated with sexual maturity and stem from the reproductive instinct. An unconscious confusion then occurs between the drives inherent in the magical dimension of love, to which the child unconsciously aspires, and the biological drives linked to reproduction, which erupt at puberty. All the impulses specific to sacred Eros, which have remained dormant for many years, then become focused on these biological drives. The instinctual energy linked to the sexual function, meant to lead to the transcendent, feeds, for lack of anything better, the brand-new drives specific to reproduction.

This confusion of drives is not without consequences: the unconscious protests in every way possible against a situation that is anything but natural—even if it is now mimeographed in seven billion copies. All the vital energy intended to fuel spiritual fulfillment is channeled into biological tasks. Existential failure, recognized by the unconscious, translates into an irrepressible feeling of frustration and guilt, triggering aggressive reaction formations characteristic of adolescence. These reactions, lacking the ability to pinpoint the true causes of failure, are directed against all sorts of scapegoats: rival, spouse, boss, competitor, dissident, homosexual, delinquent. Thus arises the latent destructiveness that characterizes our interpersonal relationships.

This inner conflict sometimes also manifests as poetic inspiration—poems, songs, plays celebrating the bliss that authentic love should envelop us in, or wallowing in self-compassion, recounting the suffering of failed romances, denouncing the routine of married life, and relegating hopes for happiness to some fantasy of paradise. The same mechanisms underlie the pornography craze, and even more pathological behaviors such as rape, sexual assault, mutilation, and other such atrocities.

Extrasensory perception and spiritual evolution are the only things capable of giving true meaning to existence. Living simply to reproduce is in itself a vicious cycle, a kind of circular argument, repeating itself with each generation without leading anywhere. The parents' "disconnection" compromises the child's "connection", and the child, now grown, perpetuates this debacle for their own offspring. We find ourselves in a world ever more enamored with material and technological progress, but it is a disenchanted world. The feeling of inner emptiness only worsens, without us understanding why.

This confusion has worsened considerably since the self-proclaimed scientists of the Enlightenment and their mechanistic heirs, following in Descartes' footsteps, attempted to explain animals by machines, and then humans by animals. Human sexuality was suddenly expected to be modeled on animal sexuality. The moralism that gradually took hold, and with which we are still imbued today, is based on the idea that the purpose of physical love is the fertilization of an egg by a sperm.

This is the leitmotif of sex education as it has been taught in schools for generations. And since virtually the entire population is subjected to the same Fordist approach, the stereotype is even more entrenched than it was at the beginning of the last century. Any transcendent dimension linked to Eros is swept aside in advance, with all the force that notions instilled during school years can carry for a lifetime.

There is, however, a glimmer of hope: three fundamental texts have been issued at the highest levels in recent years and are expected to set precedents at the UN level in the more or less near future, depending on how quickly elected officials implement them and the undoubtedly considerable resistance they will encounter: the "Declaration of Sexual Rights" (published in 1999 and then 2014) by the WAS (World Association for Sexual Health), the "Declaration of Sexual Rights" by the IPPF (Institute for Family Planning) published in 2008, and the new "Standards for Sexuality Education in Europe" by the WHO (World Health Organization), published on October 20, 2010. The latter was drafted by the "German Federal Centre for Sexual Health" (BzgA) on behalf of the WHO Regional Office for Europe.

Many recommendations contradict currently applied principles and could promote a more natural development of psychosexual structures. For example, in the section on "Sexuality": allow children from 0 to 4 years old "to express their needs and desires". Help them develop "a healthy appreciation of the feelings of well-being, closeness, and trust created by physical experience and attachment". Help them develop "a curiosity about their own body and the bodies of others". Inform them about "the pleasure and satisfaction associated with touching their own body", about "the fact that pleasure from physical contact is a normal aspect of everyone's life". Talk about "unpleasant sensations in their own body". Inform them about "tenderness and physical contact as an expression of love and affection", about "the right to explore nudity and the body, to be curious", teach them to "express their own needs", to say "yes or no".

In the section on "Sexuality, Health, and Well-being", for children aged 4 to 6: inform them about "the meaning and expression of sexuality, for example, feelings of love". Inform them about "friendship and love for people of the same sex". Allow children to "trust their instincts and apply the three-step model: say no, leave, talk to someone they trust". Enable them "to successfully experience well-being". For children aged 9 to 12: enable them to "consciously decide whether or not to have sexual experiences". Help them develop "acceptance of sexual rights for themselves and others". Inform them about "pleasure, masturbation, and orgasm". For 12- to 15-year-olds: enable adolescents to "make free and responsible choices after evaluating the consequences, advantages, and disadvantages (partners, sexual behaviors)".

One wonders what Bosch would have thought of this. Perhaps nothing in particular, given that at the time, the very notion of an age limit was not considered in matters of sexuality. Had he known the current state of affairs, he would undoubtedly have seen this as a profound moral revolution, paving the way for a rehabilitation of Adamic Eros and a new societal paradigm. All the ills he describes in his Inferno could be overcome by uprooting the endemic neurosis. Individuals structured on a model free from repression are indeed much more likely to rediscover the primary function of love and sexuality, because it is simply natural.

The moral renewal that seems to be beginning unfortunately lacks a solid theoretical framework. Yet the necessary material has been circulating for over a century. Freud attributes two distinct purposes to sexuality. He writes verbatim that there are two sexual functions in humans: one that occurs at birth and whose sole purpose is *pleasure*, and another that only appears at puberty and aims at *breeding*. According to him, the first, although largely repressed under the influence of the Oedipus complex, must, in adulthood, be *put at the service* of the second. It is then expressed as "*preliminary pleasures*". From this perspective, coitus remains the ultimate goal of the process, essential to the survival of the species, while other types of relationships, called "*polymorphic*", would constitute only so many secondary forms of arousal.

Before going further, let us note that the postulate on which Freudian decoding is based is itself already contradictory: attributing *pleasure* as the sole purpose of a sexual function proves to be nonsense, given that no instinct has pleasure as its sole objective. The hunger instinct, for example, is accompanied by a notable pleasure when eating; but this instinct does not have pleasure as its end: its ultimate goal is to nourish the body. Pleasure accompanies us as a driving force in the search for food and as a learning agent. Through pleasure, individuals learn which foods are suitable for them and will tend to seek them out for sustenance.

The same is true for all instincts. A pursuit of pleasure without any other purpose would, in itself, be contrary to the laws of evolution, because it represents a loss of energy without any worthwhile return. Such a scenario evokes the famous experiment of the rat with an electrode in its skull, stimulating its pleasure centers when it presses a pedal: the poor animal then never stops pedaling, losing all its vital energy. This type of short circuit would be impossible in nature, automatically leading to a decline, resulting in the elimination of the individual and the entire strain. The most basic evolutionary logic thus compels us to ask what, beyond pleasure, is the true purpose of this sexual function not dedicated to reproduction.

Some might argue that learning about pleasure is beneficial to the reproductive function. It then remains to explain why sexual drives are established in early childhood, well before maturity, resulting in Oedipal repression, which only serves to alter sexuality and is therefore not conducive to reproduction. It seems much more coherent to postulate that non-reproductive sexuality has a specific purpose. The problem was defining this purpose. Sexologists could have long ago found the key to the enigma in the work of Bosch. Unfortunately, the dominant paradigm places the arts and sciences in separate categories. 

Attributing a spiritual function to non-reproductive sexual drives also allows, as a secondary benefit, for the rehabilitation of homosexuality. It is explained as a natural drive aimed at gaining transcendent energy, and not as a deviation from so-called normal sexuality. Indeed, we observe that many creators, artists, and innovators are homosexual. Our postulate thus resolves a serious civilizational problem that dates back to Sodom and Gomorrah. Mosaic prohibitions and their various forms have shattered countless lives and produced immeasurable suffering under the guise of divine will. It is terrifying to think that so many tragedies were caused by the simple ignorance of a natural law.

Another major consequence of our postulate is that it provides a coherent explanation for the mystery of "original sin". The starting point of the Old Testament evokes a sexual relationship: Adam and Eve yield to the serpent's temptations and eat the forbidden fruit, dragging all their descendants into the Fall. Our entire Judeo-Christian culture thus rests on a paradox: the act necessary for the survival of the species leads to divine condemnation. The biblical commandment "Be fruitful and multiply" threatens us today with a population explosion that would be fatal to the planet.

For ages, theologians have engaged in endless discourses on the necessity of Evil to achieve Good, of Sin to attain Redemption, without ever fundamentally resolving the enigma of Genesis. Only the Virgin Mary, the ultimate escape, had the chance to procreate without succumbing to sin. No one can tell us how she did it, nor what actually happened. For half a millennium, Bosch has offered us a conception of love that could have fundamentally changed everything. Sin no longer lies in obeying the desires of the body; it is the dictates of moralizers that thwart sacred Eros and condemn us to sin.

Like anything that involves a paradigm shift, the message of The *Garden of Earthly Delights* has encountered all sorts of resistance, both conscious and unconscious, which explains the general incomprehension among commentators. It's even surprising that it has managed to survive the centuries while remaining perfectly enigmatic. Perhaps because the unconscious senses its depth, without the conscious mind being able to grasp its very essence. It will be interesting to observe how this message, translated into intelligible terms, will be received by our contemporaries.

The simple fact of positing that non-reproductive sexuality serves the purpose of developing extrasensory abilities provides an answer to another crucial question: why are these abilities so rare in our modern society? The blame lies quite simply with the pervasiveness of the Oedipus complex. The aspect of sexuality aimed at developing extrasensory perception is nipped in the bud, in the population as a whole. Consequently, individuals capable of developing any kind of mediumship are extremely rare, to the point that we no longer know what the paranormal should represent.

This, however, does not hold true when we have absolutely no control over a future portion of history, over a chain of uncontrollable events, for example, a house that is going to collapse without any warning, or a meteorite impact: in such cases, the dangerous event can be foretold to us. This will allow us to avoid becoming a victim ourselves, and to prevent it for others, but not to prevent the event itself. The supposed internal contradiction of precognition is therefore nothing more than a bubble inflated with reductionism.

One more word on the concept of energy-information. Derived from the laws of matter, this concept may seem inappropriate when dealing with phenomena that transcend matter, such as anything of a spiritual or extrasensory nature. Yet, it is justifiable like any other fundamental quantity. The physicist—the most scientific of scientists—does no better when considering the energy of a material system. This energy manifests itself through its capacity to activate a system. It is characterized by the processes of transmission, accumulation, transformation, and degeneration into heat. However, it only appears through its mechanical, electromagnetic, chemical, or thermal manifestations and remains unknowable in itself.

When you pedal a bicycle, your chemically derived energy (the glucose burned in your muscles) is transformed into mechanical energy, partly into kinetic energy because you are accelerating your body and your bicycle, and partly into potential energy if you are climbing a hill. A portion of this energy, however, is transformed into heat: you sweat, which represents calories; the hubs of your wheels, your tires, and your rims—provided your brakes were left slightly engaged—all these components of the system experience a temperature increase. A small portion of the initial chemical energy has "degenerated" into thermal energy, in the sense that it is not entirely recoverable. The concept of "energy" underpins the entire argument and lends itself to precise calculations. Yet, it is impossible to define it, that is, to reduce it to other, more general concepts. It's a bit like when geometry encounters axioms, propositions that we are forced to accept without being able to prove them. This is why the physicist's energy is considered a "fundamental quantity".

The analogy with the energy generated in a non-reproductive romantic relationship is striking: like real nourishment, this energy manifests itself at the level of motivation, creative intuition, and the capacity for action (setting things in motion), in the direction of existential or spiritual progress (climbing a ramp). It accumulates in the body (like potential or kinetic energy), is transmitted from one individual to another (like movement or electricity), and can degenerate into suffering (like heat can destroy). But the concept itself remains indefinable; like the physicist's concept of energy, it constitutes a fundamental quantity.

On an analogous level, it's worth noting that overheating finds its perfect counterpart. The rise in temperature isn't necessarily useless: if a cyclist burns their fingers touching their rims, they become aware of a mistake, for example, a poorly adjusted brake that caused friction and slowed their progress. They can then correct this error and avoid a potential accident. Similarly, when there is friction, disagreements between partners in a romantic relationship, or inner dispositions detrimental to an individual's development (ego, desire, jealousy, hatred, attachment, self-pity, etc.), the energy that should allow progress on a spiritual level ends up degenerating into suffering. Just like physical pain, this moral suffering compels us to question ourselves, to try to understand our mistakes, and to correct them. This quest, insofar as it leads to overcoming temptation, represents a gain in consciousness. In this sense, it is said that "one must suffer to evolve".

This is true as long as the suffering remains limited. When it exceeds our capacity for resilience, because we are slow to recognize our mistakes and renounce them, it can also destroy us, like excessive heat. We then risk succumbing to ego, bitterness, hatred, rejection of the spiritual, or even suicide. The worst outcome is inner hardening, which is the exact opposite of spiritual evolution. Bosch masterfully depicted these different manifestations, notably in the Hell of The *Garden of Earthly Delights*.

It is clear that these essential mechanisms malfunction within the dominant cultural context. Trapped in a paradigm that condemns both the extrasensory and spiritual functions of love, we are placed in a position where it is almost impossible to trace the root causes of our suffering. Nothing allows us to assess the consequences of the loss of sacred Eros, since we no longer have the slightest idea of ​​them. As a result, suffering most often exceeds in intensity and duration the threshold we are capable of enduring, and ultimately destroys us. The detours we should take through pain to become aware of the causes of our errors, the inanity of the ego, and the vanity of our desires, reach such proportions that we lose faith in the transcendent dimension of existence. We take refuge in the mind, in hardening our stance, in blame, in indifference, in depression, if not in alcohol, drugs, or suicidal impulses—paradoxically, in everything that is contrary to our spiritual evolution. This picture, presented to us without ambiguity in Bosch's Hell, in The *Garden of Earthly Delights* as in *The Haywain*, is omnipresent. It is presented, subtly, as inseparable from our repressive, materialistic, and reductionist culture.

Everything would be different if the experience of visions and archetypes were shared by all. But this is far from being the case. On the contrary, an underlying anathema reigns, preventing us from objectively examining these questions. The witch hunts of the Middle Ages still persist, in a latent form, in the guise of skepticism, accusations of superstition, and mistrust of anything that might be considered extrasensory. Any questioning of sexual morality is suspected of perversion, any attempt at liberation is considered heresy. This is the direct legacy of centuries when courts, under the pretext of witch hunts and fueled by collective hysteria, tortured poor women to force them to confess to diabolical orgies and condemned them to the stake. The new deity of the modern world, christened Science, has failed to restore to us, in these areas, our natural freedom of thought and action. It is itself trapped by reductionism and incapable of penetrating any reality that transcends the material. Sexology should be an exception, but it too obscures the transcendent dimension of love and proves more toxic than liberating.

Now imagine that you had visions: they would immediately bring you, in symbolic form, the answers you seek. They would allow you, day after day, to get to the heart of difficult situations, to solve problems infinitely faster, and to escape suffering that would then become pointless. To restore the synchronicity between your actions and your destiny, and its hallmark, which is happiness.

Bosch is particularly harsh on the clergy. He denounces, through this, the failure of religions to restore to us the transcendent values ​​they allow us to glimpse. Most religions advocate sexual morality as a guarantee of salvation. This is undoubtedly because the early Church Fathers who forged their rites and creeds sensed the importance of love in spiritual evolution and the gravity of its lustful excesses. The measures they took have unfortunately done more harm than good because, by failing to distinguish between the two functions of love, they have sown disgrace upon all sexuality and further confused minds.

Moses wrote in the seventh commandment of the Decalogue, "You shall not commit adultery". In Deuteronomy, he advocated the death penalty for adultery, as well as for homosexuality. These are undoubtedly the commandments that have been most readily transgressed and most cruelly punished throughout the millennia. How can we know whether the evolution of morals that trivializes these two offenses today represents progress or rather a deviation?

The question finds an answer immediatiately if we posit the existence of two sexual functions: homosexuality and adultery are unnatural in terms of reproduction, while they can constitute a fundamental source of energy in terms of sacred sex. If we are to believe Bosch, the error lies in confining love-energy to the couple and seeking happiness in coitus. This indirectly leads to spiritual death. The necessary energies, which repetitive sexual intercourse cannot provide or merely wastes, are no longer sufficient to fuel extrasensory perception and spiritual fulfillment. The enormous void then leads to straying into all sorts of substitute impulses, Hyperactivity, overthinking, moralizing, guilt, anxiety, jealousy, possessiveness, depression, despair, romantic infatuations, perverse fantasies, hatred, murderous fantasies, sexual obsessions, even rape and all that follows.

In a binary context, the idea inevitably arises that the love one experiences is intrinsically dependent on the presence of the other partner. Impulses become fixated on that person as the sole source of love. Thus, the fear of losing the object of love, dependence, possessiveness, exclusion of others, and jealousy automatically emerge.

Daily experience of the interplay between love and extrasensory perception shows that open relationships, on the contrary, constitute a privileged source of transcendent energy. Simply being aware of the existence of transcendent energy drawn from the loving relationship completely changes the respective positions of the protagonists. The partner is perceived as participating in a natural, sacred rite, aiming at eternal values ​​far beyond individual concerns. The third party, for its part, represents a potential source of additional energy, the very opposite of a threat or any kind of loss. Love is no longer felt as something to be defended against foreign intrusion, but as a miraculous gift that multiplies when shared. It is experienced in an "oblative", unconditional way, like a kind of offering made to the divine. This offering is rewarded by the fullness of bliss and by the grace of archetypal messages capable of giving meaning and direction to our destinies.

This original functioning of love develops not only openness toward one's partner, but much more broadly toward others, toward the material world, toward the cosmos in general. It constitutes the source of a quality essential to living together: empathy, through a daily learning of sensitivity to the suffering of others. Any suffering for which one feels responsible immediately causes the magic of love to vanish, perceived as a loss of happiness, whereas every moment of openness, selflessness, or the giving of love multiplies feelings of fulfillment and ecstasy. Closeness or indifference toward others becomes synonymous with a loss of love, the exact opposite of what occurs under normal circumstances.

In every triangular relationship, there is at least one same-sex partner, and it is apparently this homosexual relationship that constitutes the source of transcendent energy. The facts show that visions only appear within a heterosexual relationship when it is not repetitive, and provided that at least one partner is fueled by the energy drawn from a relationship with a third party of the same sex. This type of relationship, experienced with subtlety, should obviously not be confused with the image of the "gay" couple, a derisory copy of the heterosexual couple, nor with that of the leather-clad gay man armed with a whip, a parody of the macho heterosexual.

It's easy to understand why he directed the stigmatization toward a pole close to the one it should have targeted.

Yet Genesis traces the curse of the chosen people back to the disobedience of a heterosexual couple, not a homosexual couple. Moses clearly overlooked this detail…
