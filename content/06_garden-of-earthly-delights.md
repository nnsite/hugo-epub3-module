At first glance, this interpretation might seem acrobatic. We will see that it is corroborated by each of the details staged throughout the triptych. It reflects the patent filiation between the owl in the center of paradise, and the growing owl here at the point of convergence of this sibyllin procession. Moreover, it is in perfect agreement with the experience of the transcendent function of love and the development of extrasensory perception, as I will relate in the second part of the book.

Bosch had a definite intention by placing this bird loaded with meaning where the viewer's attention would converge. Seeing motifs so rich and puzzling leading to a frail animal barely feathered obviously disconcertes the viewer up to the best analyst, as all ignore does not know that some form of *sacred wedding* develops paranormal faculties. Bewildering the viewer was the best way to lead him to spark questioning.

The foreground is animated by many groups with both innocent and busy looks. Fraenger described this area with multiple games in love as the "pedagogical province", a name we gladly conserve, as we will discover there a true teaching dedicated to the things of love.

From the central motif of the garden of delights, we find the existence of the two forms of Eros dear to Plato, one that leads to procreation, and the other to spiritual evolution. This must, however, be nuanced, knowing that the great philosopher opposed a Uranian Eros, only pederastic, allowing to reach the Essences, to a Pandemic or vulgar Eros, aimed only at pleasure – or reproduction in case of heterosexual relationship.

![](Yfruit.png)As with the giant duck's gougard astonishment, the alternative is highlighted by a reversed character whose legs form a Y. The hands' placement proves this concerns sex, to the point that Fraenger saw in this motif a condemnation without appeal of masturbation: the solitary vice would have been the cause of a tragic blindness, of a reversal of the values the Free Spirit attributed to sacred sexuality.
{.x-50}

The Berlin historian clearly did not know masturbation only started to be condemned from the mid-18th century, following a loud campaign by moralists and doctors, who had nothing particularly Catholic. The Church condemned it only at the beginning of the next century, no doubt so as not to be vilified by secular morality. We find no word on this subject in the minutes of the trial of the Free Spirit, implying it was simply *not mentioned, either by the sect itself or inquisitorial judges*. Experience never showed masturbation itself harms sacred Eros, it is rather *masturbative fantasies* that can cause feelings of guilt or install certain sexual addictions. Under these conditions, it is difficult to subscribe to Fraenger's point of view.

Another interpretation seems more plausible: The Y traditionally symbolizes free will. Bosch thus describes the possibility we have of choosing between one of the two tracks. One of man's feet touches the amniotic pocket, while the other refers to the scene of blackberry and floating orange. Parallel to the left leg, a perforating spine unambiguously evokes penetration, the bird perched on the spine evokes the incarnation of a soul. This is clearly the path of procreation. The right leg, guiding our eyes towards the blackberry, lies parallel to a sacred ibis housed in a notch. The volutes of a trilobed branch reminds us of the link between Knowledge and the subtle energy represented by the nurturing blackberry. The pearls also evoke orgasm here. It is an accomplished sexuality, though without penetration, a source of transcendent energy fuelling spiritual evolution.

![](oiseau_pendu_bas.jpg)This second path requires more than the other one an initiatory teaching, as symbolizes it, like the lavish procession of giant birds arriving from the left, the tit suspended from the upper leaf of thistle. The giant blackberry is commensurate with the universal need (all these hungry mouths) of an energy accessible through this form of Eros, allowing to reach the sacred in bliss.

On the contrary, reproduction seems to be a burden, a responsibility and a source of concern. ![](tier_inquiet.png)The face visible in the tube symbolizing penetration expresses the impulses this type of contact, closer to animality, automatically arouses in the third party through his serious impavidness. These are the same impulses that, exacerbated, can lead the "aggrieved third person" to murder, or the group to punish adultery of death. Their unconscious purpose in itself is to preserve the spiritual dimension of the relationship. Their intensity is proportional to the importance of the issue of the risk of spiritual failure associated with a degraded form of love. They can become deadly when failure is systematic to the point where there is deep anguish of spiritual failure. The semantic proximity to typical animal pulsions of competition between males further exacerbates them.

They transform inversely in a sense of amorous magic and respect for the sacred when love is lived on the mode of celestial Eros. The feeling of amorous magic, of eternity related to love, is actually an indicator of the presence of transcendent energy. One feels this amorous magic in the solar plexus, distinct from normal romantic feelings most often carrying hearts towards inextricable and painful disappointed love stories.

In the particular case, the scrutinizing look of the third person in its tube reflects the necessary vigilance in the face of the possible tribulations of the reproducting couple, required to safeguard the magical dimension that be associated with coitus.

As I will point out in [chapter V](thirdeye) of my personal history, and unlike Plato who ranks concupiscence and reproduction in the tote of the Pandemic Eros (or vulgar Eros), I do believe I can actually distinguish two modes of reproduction. A purely biological one, devoid of amorous magic and subtle energy, even charged with *stupre* and aggressiveness. The other one carried by the transcendent dimension, requiring an innocent and non-possessive approach to love that allows for a supply of energy by a third person. The bearer of the mussel in which the reproductive couple is located looks at us with an exhausted air to inform us that such a union must be carried by a third person, which presupposes the absence of possessivity, jealousy, and guilt. This implies shedding beyond one's ego. Knowing that this self-actualization is the sine qua non condition of spiritual evolution, the loop is closed.

The purely binary and genital reproduction (the most common pandemic Eros) is not represented here. According to Bosch, *it does not exist in heaven* and instead leads to the hell of the third panel. She is the president of marital hell – under which the first part of my childhood took place. The loving malaise of parents, the existential emptiness of the closed couple, the *lack of a deep connection between the adults' destiny and that of the child* create frustrations and aggressive impulses that are difficult to control. This is where all the problems of our modern families take root, whose mechanisms Psychoanalysis attempted to inventory and decrypt. Unfortunately, it lacked the notions of sacred Eros or transcendent energy, without which one cannot unravel the mystery. Yet Bosch succeeded in doing this more than four centuries ago, as we will discover in Hell of this triptych and in the *Haywain Tryptich*.

But the inaugural lesson, which concerns daily life, is highlighted right next to this trio of hungry people. Some interpreters once again see a transfigured couple (even Fraenger). It would be naive to put on the back of a pictorial imperity the apparent left-handedness of this pattern. Bosch knows how to draw and draw with perfect fit in expressions and movements when he so desires. The clumsiness he depicts in this motive clearly intended to underline the ineptitude of the underlying situation. He who has the experience of the triangular constellation will recognize both the deliberate will of the painter, and the relevance of the lesson.

The later articulates in ordinary language as such: "*Every time you want to own the partner, a third person wrecked*". That is to say, we must learn non-possessive, non-exclusive love, free from desire, and open to third parties. The woman's slap perfectly reflects the impulses of visceral opposition arising in the face of male desire, of the same order as those of the character looking for the mouse penetrating into the crystal tube. The intensity of these impulses is explained by the gravity of the stakes, the failure of the celestial Eros leading to the gravissim loss of extrasensory perception and spiritual achievement.

*The fact is that there is always a third, if not several, in every love story of some length*. Everyone inevitably tends to move from one relationship to another, and this can be done in two ways: either by rejecting the new partner to safeguard the old relationship; or by repudiating the former partner to fly away in holy matrimony with the newcomer. Both solutions lead to failure. In the first case, the renunciation of the love momentum one of the partners can feel for the third person inevitably generates bitterness, budding resent against the one to which one strives to remain faithful. The best effort of will will not prevent the relationship allegedly salvaged from being tarnished by a sense of frustration, all the more painful as the new stillborn love would have been provided an essential source of energy for the first partners. In the second case, it is the breakup with the former partner that leaves sequels in the form of feelings of guilt in the leaving party, and jealousy or reproach in the one left stranded.

In both cases, the superior function of love pays the price. We cannot build an innocent love on the back of the suffering of others. Any feeling of guilt destroys amorous magic, and without amorous magic, the most beautiful relationship quickly falls into boredom, inner emptiness and reproaches. Virtually all couples experience it, more or less tragic depending on the circumstances.

Here, the process is even more injunctive. It is not an ordinary couple relationship, a contract of good understanding condoned by the mayor or the priest. The challenge is to carry out a form of subtle love, supposed to lead to the "miracle" represented by the *development of extrasensory faculties*. Let us therefore take these first two lessons: to know that love impulses reflect the quest for an energy of transcendent nature, and to know this relationship must be established outside any possessivity. Only on this double condition can we move on to the following lessons.

The lesson here is also perfectly clear: one of the two hands draws attention to the bird's head, while the other designates its solar plexus. Translated into ordinary language: the key to the problem is the relationship between what happens in the head and what happens in the solar plexus. We recognize the rule of "non-thinking" dear to tantrists, verified many times in practice: any investment in the mind, which in fact is always related to the ego, blocks the celestial Eros. We must know how to stay focused on the level of the solar plexus, in an attitude of openness, questioning and obedience to the messages delivered by the intuitions, the feeling of amorous magic, and especially *visions* through archetypal symbols.

The pattern of the shipwreck, towards which the procession of birds converges, teaches us that non-possessive love is the first step in this journey. The occupants of pineapple and orange were saved from the waters, by the miraculous effect of the awakening put in the image in thistle. The characters still immersed enumerate the various keys of rescue: the centration of consciousness in the solar plexus, the transcendent energy symbolized by the giant blackberry and the small blackberry of the big-bec. The legs in Y remind us that *we have free will*, and that it is up to us to choose our way.

Note that the fruitful union illustrated in the amniotic pocket is what one might call a "assisted reproduction": the copulative mussel carried by a third person, which does not spare its efforts and harkens back to triangular dynamics; the physical proceedings are duly controlled by someone outside the couple who does not seem to enjoy himself one bit. The main thing remains a necessary input of energy, symbolized by the giant blackberry on the shoulder of a carrier leaning forward parallel to the foothold of the mussel. The abnormally tense right leg calls parallels that of the upside-down man pointing at the mother mulberry emerging from orange.

Bosch thus insists on the primacy of energy not only to guarantee the awakening that promotes access to the sacred Eros, but to give back the reproductive dimension its sacrality. The bird takes its flight in the exact vertical axis of the solar plexus of the owl postiche: a soul is ready to incarnate following the union of the sexes, provided that it is carried by a transcendent energy, whose place of implantation par excellence is this center today forgotten.

Additional detail: the flight of the bird, alias the incarnation of a soul, depends on two conditions: the supply of transcendent energy (symbolized by the blackberry), guided by a "plugged-in" wisdom (the trilobed branch to which the bearer stands), and the fact of only concerning oneself with energy, represented by the red fruit on the head of the colored woman (instead of a deliberate quest for carnal pleasure).

Let's look again at the proximity of the kundalinî (the giant strawberry on the initiate's back, on the same horizontal line as the blackberry and the red fruit). This function of metapsychic essence also plays an essential role on the path of inspired, righteous procreation, because its purpose is to indicate, at every moment of doubt, whether one embarks on the right path. It alone can direct our free will and free our choices from the vicissitudes of chance.

The red fruits with which the partners seem to juggle perfectly harmoniously clearly represent a nourishing energy that they are exchanging and multiplying. At the very moment of an erotic relationship, the prevalence of physical and emotional sensations prevails and must prevail over all other feelings.

To the exegetes who saw in this scene a representation of concupiscence and sin, one can immediately answer they are mistaken: the corolla indicates on the contrary partners do not boggle themselves in mental ideas, therefore not in desire, being none other than the calculated search for pleasure. There is therefore a perfect overlap with the owl postiche (centering in the solar plexus and not in the mind), as well as with the nuptial couple covered with a red hood preceding the little owl at the end of the procession.

Some others wanted to see in these two dancers an ideal couple, in the midst of celebration of sacred weddings. We can't give them any reason either. We see, just below the couple, a third character, who nourishes his soul with the same energy that exchanges between the other two. Important detail: it presents the fruit to a bird perched on its foot, stating that this energy must be used not to feed the ego, but the soul, the spiritual dimension of the being. In these circumstances, it is often in this third partner that the implantation of visions and other extrasensory abilities most strongly manifests, as I have seen throughout the unusual adventures told in the last chapters.

Note that at this stage, the interpretation of the four owls marking the two panels appears perfectly consistent. They can be declared the main key to deciphering the encrypted message. The one seen in the centre of Paradise is of the same size, although younger, as a goal of sacred union represented by the couple encapsulated in the middle of the central panel: it is love that ensures the development of extrasensory faculties, provided that sexuality obeys the rules set out through the different singularities of the groups of characters. The two great owls of the pedagogic province come to explain for one the imperative rule of thinking and centration in the solar plexus, involving the absence of any concupiscence, for the other the importance of the triangular constellation and the affinity between partners in the sacred union of bodies.

The extrasensory faculties are clearly evoked around the owl of paradise: the clairvoyance, represented by the giant liris on which lies the Fountain of Life, and the Kundalinî, figurated by the immense column that sneezes towards the sky. These two inseparable functions, whose forgotten power Bosch reminds us of, give access to **true Knowledge**, to which all the efforts of human beings tend without their knowing, his vision having been obliterated by the lyssek, that is, by sexuality in its reproductive biological function.

So, one of the main puzzles of the triptych seems resolved. Cross-checking with the many other symbols that remain to be elucidated will confirm it beyond doubt.
